UN COP31 Climate Talks End Without Deal Amid Deep Divisions

The UN COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, has ended without a major new agreement after negotiations broke down over fossil fuels and climate finance. Running late into Sunday, the talks became the longest UN climate conference in history, but ultimately failed to secure a consensus cover text following deep divisions between participating nations.

The final draft text became unacceptable to the European Union, Pacific island nations, Panama, and other countries because it omitted direct mentions of fossil fuels and failed to provide clear financial commitments from wealthy nations to help poorer countries adapt to rising temperatures. Critics argued that the proposed text lacked the necessary ambition and effectively greenwashed the issue, with European representatives and climate advocates expressing strong dismay over the outcome.

Conversely, oil-producing nations and other countries resisted stricter mandates on coal, oil, and gas. Delegates from regions including Saudi Arabia and Russia argued that they account for a smaller proportion of historical emissions and warned that moving away from fossil fuels too quickly could lead to fuel poverty. The collapse marks the first time a main political agreement at a COP has collapsed since Copenhagen in 2009.

  • The EU, Panama, and Pacific island nations rejected the draft deal, while oil-producing and other nations resisted rapid fossil fuel phase-outs.
  • UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres expressed deep disappointment over the outcome.

Russian Warship Sunk in Suspected Ukrainian Drone Attack in Libya

Ukrainian naval drones targeted and sank a Russian warship docked at the port of Benghazi in eastern Libya on Sunday, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and verification from satellite imagery and intelligence firms. Zelenskyy stated that seven Ukrainian-made naval drones were deployed in the operation, successfully destroying the Russian corvette Merkury—one of Russia's newest ships—as well as a Ka-27 combat helicopter. Ukrainian officials also claimed that a second Russia-linked vessel, identified by Kyiv as a container carrier or cargo ship used for transporting weapons, was struck off the coast.

The attack marks a notable geographic expansion of the conflict into the Mediterranean, more than 1,000 miles from southern Ukraine. The warship had reportedly docked at the Benghazi port to refuel and take on supplies ahead of a return to Russia. Libyan authorities in the east, who control the port and are aligned with Russia, reported that several drones were intercepted before two managed to strike the warship, triggering a major explosion and fire. The eastern-based administration condemned the incident as a violation of Libyan sovereignty and a threat to maritime safety, emphasizing that Libya is not a party to the Russia-Ukraine conflict.

International reactions followed the incident, with the United Nations mission in Libya (UNSMIL) expressing serious concern over the drone attacks and calling for an immediate, independent, and transparent investigation to prevent Libya from becoming a battleground for foreign conflicts. Meanwhile, the internationally recognized government in Tripoli warned against hosting foreign forces and involving local ports in external disputes. Moscow has not yet issued an official comment on the attack.

South Korean Education Minister Resigns Following Exam Cheating Scandal and Emergency Measures

South Korea's Education Minister, Choi Kyo-jin, has resigned following a nationwide exam cheating scandal and subsequent public backlash over last-minute preventive measures. The controversy erupted after the top scorer in a national tax accountancy exam confessed to using a miniature camera, smartphone, and artificial intelligence tools to achieve a near-perfect score. This confession rattled the country just days before the Suneung, South Korea's high-stakes annual college entrance exam that heavily influences students' future career paths and social mobility.

In response to the growing fears of academic dishonesty, government officials scrambled to implement urgent anti-cheating measures. These included sending nationwide emergency alerts to mobile phones during the exam, blocking public Wi-Fi in testing areas, doubling the number of supervisors, inspecting eyewear, and restricting clothing with buttons. While the exam concluded without major incidents, the disruptive alerts and hasty implementation drew heavy criticism from the public, teachers' unions, and honest test-takers who felt paranoid or inadequately prepared for the sudden rules.

Minister Choi faced intense scrutiny not only for the belated preparation but also for remarks during a parliamentary hearing where he expressed a lack of confidence in his ability to catch cheaters due to his age. Acknowledging the public confusion and accepting full responsibility for failing to prepare in advance, Choi announced his resignation on Sunday. President Lee Jae Myung subsequently accepted the resignation, as the government continues to face broader calls for an overhaul of the current exam system.

A devastating fire broke out on Saturday evening at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia confectionery factory in Cikande, Banten province, located about 50 kilometers west of Jakarta. Search and rescue operations faced significant challenges due to collapsed structures, poor lighting conditions, and production materials that threatened to reignite the blaze. Kerala High Court Sets Aside Suspension of 16 Sanskrit University Students

The Kerala High Court has set aside the suspension of 16 students belonging to the Students Federation of India (SFI) at the Sree Sankaracharya University of Sanskrit (SSUS) in Kalady. The court directed the university to immediately reinstate the students and arrange special examinations for them within six weeks. The students had previously faced an 84-day suspension following protests on campus on August 13, during which they were accused of staging a mock cremation of Vice-Chancellor Ciza Thomas, manhandling her, and attempting to damage her property.

The students completely denied the allegations, pointing out that the inquiry committee report failed to state reasons for finding them guilty and noting that a dissenting committee member had stated none of the students were involved in misconduct. They challenged both the suspension order and the subsequent inquiry committee report through an amended petition, arguing that their shortage of attendance and internal marks was entirely due to the prolonged suspension. While the university opposed their petition on grounds of insufficient attendance and internal marks, the High Court allowed the challenge and ruled in favor of the students.

The court's decision sparked celebrations on campus and brought an end to a 22-day relay hunger strike by the students. Political leaders and student organizations supporting the students hailed the verdict as a triumph of truth and student unity against political agendas, while student representatives asserted they would now return to classes and write their examinations.
