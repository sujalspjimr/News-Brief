Youth-Led Protests Over Cost of Living and Education Turn Violent in France and Germany

Hundreds of thousands of demonstrators took to the streets across France and Germany on Saturday to protest rising living costs, underfunded public services, and educational conditions. While the daytime rallies were largely peaceful and youth-led, both nations saw significant unrest and clashes with police as darkness fell, resulting in hundreds of arrests and multiple injuries.

In France, an estimated 320,000 people participated in a third consecutive weekend of nationwide demonstrations, including approximately 70,000 in Paris. Marches uniting students, trade unions, and "yellow vest" demonstrators protested government economic policies and declining public infrastructure. Later in the evening, clashes erupted in Paris, Lyon, and Toulouse, where masked groups threw paving stones and incendiary devices, prompting security forces to respond with tear gas, stun grenades, and water cannon. French authorities reported that at least five security personnel were injured, with one officer struck by a molotov cocktail.

In Germany, rallies organized by the youth group "Gen Z regelt" brought tens of thousands into the streets in cities such as Berlin, Frankfurt, Munich, and Cologne to oppose public spending cuts, education shortfalls, and military service. Nighttime unrest escalated in Frankfurt, where rioters smashed shop windows, looted stores—including a gun shop where weapons were seized—and threw molotov cocktails at emergency workers. Similar skirmishes occurred in Cologne and Hamburg, leading police to deploy water cannon.

Officials in both countries condemned the violence. French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez condemned an "extraordinary" level of violence from rioters, while Hesse Interior Minister Roman Poseck attributed the unrest in Frankfurt to left-wing extremist infiltration, warning that France's unrest must not serve as a model. Demonstrations over similar economic grievances were also reported in Italy and Albania as France prepares for an upcoming general strike.

Key facts

  • More than 320,000 people demonstrated across France, including 70,000 in Paris, leading to between 290 and 565 arrests nationwide.
  • French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez reported that an officer was hit by a molotov cocktail in Paris, an incident he characterized as attempted murder.
  • In Germany, youth-led protests organized by 'Gen Z regelt' addressed high living costs, public spending cuts, education underfunding, and compulsory military service.
  • Rioters in Frankfurt looted stores, smashed a gun shop's windows, and threw molotov cocktails, prompting the deployment of special police units.
  • Clashes and police use of water cannon or tear gas were also reported in German cities including Cologne, Berlin, and Hamburg, as well as French cities such as Lyon and Toulouse.

Civilians Evacuated From Russian-Occupied Oleshky Reunite With Families in Ukraine

Hundreds of Ukrainian civilians have escaped the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky, crossing into northern Ukraine through Belarus to reunite with family members after years of separation. The evacuation was enabled by a temporary humanitarian ceasefire across the front-line Kherson region, which took effect earlier in the week following diplomatic mediation involving U.S. First Lady Melania Trump alongside appeals from Pope Leo XIV.

Evacuees endured an arduous journey to reach Ukrainian-held territory. Despite Ukrainian military lines sitting just five kilometers from Oleshky, departing civilians had to travel across Russian-occupied territory into southern Russia, fly into Belarus, and cross by bus into Ukraine's Chernihiv region. Welcomed by relatives, local villagers, and humanitarian organizations including the Red Cross, Médecins Sans Frontières, and World Central Kitchen, arriving residents included individuals requiring stretchers and wheelchairs, with ages ranging from a one-year-old infant to a 94-year-old.

Those arriving described dire conditions during their prolonged confinement between front lines. With Oleshky cut off by Russian forces and the Dnipro River, residents faced frequent drone attacks, artillery shelling, and severe shortages of food, water, and electricity. Many reported surviving on garden weeds or navigating minefields to search for supplies. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed that evacuees would receive government support, while observers noted that some civilians opted to remain behind to protect their homes and pets.

Key facts

  • Evacuees travelled from Russian-occupied Oleshky through southern Russia and Belarus before arriving in Ukraine's Chernihiv region.
  • The evacuation was facilitated by a localized humanitarian ceasefire in Kherson brokered by U.S. First Lady Melania Trump and supported by appeals from Pope Leo XIV.
  • Residents had survived under severe shortages of food, water, and electricity, contending with frequent drone strikes, shelling, and minefields.
  • Evacuees ranged in age from one to 94 years old, with many requiring wheelchairs or stretchers upon arrival.
  • An estimated 1,700 to 2,000 people remained in Oleshky prior to the truce, and some chose to stay behind to look after homes and animals.

Binance Covers 90% of Lost Funds After FBI Attributes $6.3 Billion Theft to North Korea

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance announced it has replaced 90% of the $6.3 billion in assets stolen during a recent cyberattack, pledging that users will not absorb the financial impact. The exchange stated it set aside more than $6.34 billion in company resources to replenish accounts, drawing on its own corporate treasury, its Futures Insurance Fund, and part of its $1 billion Secure Asset Fund for Users. The remaining 10% of tokens are less liquid and will take longer to acquire, but Binance reiterated its commitment to covering the entirety of the losses.

The breach is considered the largest cryptocurrency theft on record, surpassing the $1.5 billion heist on Bybit in 2025. Following the intrusion, Binance temporarily halted customer withdrawals before initiating a phased resumption starting with major tokens such as bitcoin, ethereum, XRP, and dogecoin. The company also reported freezing roughly $3 billion in stolen assets alongside law enforcement, intelligence firms, and industry partners.

On Saturday, the FBI formally attributed the attack to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, specifically identifying a cyber unit known as TraderTraitor, an entity associated with the Lazarus Group. Investigators noted that the perpetrators quickly converted stolen holdings into ethereum and are anticipated to launder the proceeds through decentralized finance and instant swap mechanisms. North Korea has historically denied involvement in such cyberattacks.

Key facts

  • Binance allocated over $6.34 billion in company funds and has replenished 90% of the stolen assets, with the remaining 10% consisting of less liquid tokens.
  • The FBI formally blamed North Korea and the hacking entity TraderTraitor (associated with the Lazarus Group) for the $6.3 billion theft.
  • Binance froze around $3 billion of the stolen assets with the help of law enforcement, intelligence firms, and private sector partners.
  • User withdrawals, which were suspended after the breach, began reopening in phases covering cryptocurrencies such as bitcoin, ether, XRP, and dogecoin.
  • Binance's investigation indicated the attack involved a compromised engineer's laptop, additional breach points, and software using a self-destruct mechanism to erase evidence.

In favour / positives

  • Binance moved quickly to insulate customers by deploying corporate reserves and emergency funds to absorb 100% of the financial loss, already covering 90%. BBC News The Guardian DW
  • Cooperation between Binance, the US Secret Service, and blockchain partners succeeded in freezing roughly $3 billion of the stolen funds. BBC News

Against / concerns

Indian Opposition Holds Major Rally in New Delhi Over Voter Roll Revisions

Tens of thousands of people gathered in New Delhi for a major opposition protest at Ramlila Maidan, demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Organised by the opposition INDIA bloc and led by Congress party leader Rahul Gandhi, the rally followed mounting anger over a Special Intensive Revision exercise that removed 130 million names from draft electoral rolls. The opposition accused the election chief of conspiring to manipulate elections and deliberately disenfranchising eligible voters, alleging the changes disproportionately impact minorities and favour Prime Minister Narendra Modi's governing Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).

The Election Commission of India and the BJP have strongly denied the allegations, maintaining that the revision was designed to update voter lists by removing individuals who have died, moved, or could not be traced. Officials stated that wrongly deleted voters can re-enrol, and the ECI dismissed internal disagreements among commissioners as normal institutional friction. Meanwhile, the BJP launched its own counter-event in West Delhi, criticising the timing of the opposition rally for coinciding with the anniversary of the 1984 anti-Sikh riots and accusing Congress of trying to drown out the memory of those victims.

Authorities deployed thousands of police and paramilitary personnel alongside riot control vehicles to secure the protest route and manage traffic diversions in the capital. The demonstration also coincided with the implementation of Delhi's strict winter pollution curbs, which include vehicle bans and construction suspensions to tackle seasonal smog. Analysts have noted that the ongoing youth-led and opposition protests represent a significant political challenge for Prime Minister Modi's administration, though the government remains stable with the next general election scheduled for 2029.
