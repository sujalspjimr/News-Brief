Cyclonic storm Murasu, which struck Andhra Pradesh in the early hours of Tuesday, has weakened into a deep depression and subsequently into a depression while moving across east Telangana and coastal Andhra Pradesh. Karnataka Government Increases Bus Fares Across State Transport Corporations

The Karnataka government has announced a hike in bus fares across all four state-run transport corporations—BMTC, KSRTC, NWKRTC, and KKRTC—effective from midnight on November 4. The average fare increase is approximately 23% for KSRTC and ranges between 33% and 35% for BMTC. The revised pricing structure impacts ordinary, express, long-distance, and airport services, including a rise in the minimum stage-wise BMTC city bus fare from ₹6 to ₹10.

Transport Minister Byrathi Suresh stated that the decision was necessary to ensure the financial sustainability of the transport corporations, which collectively incur annual losses of around ₹2,000 crore. He attributed the revision to rising diesel prices, increasing expenditures on spare parts and vehicle maintenance, and the necessity of wage revisions for employees. Minister Suresh also noted that profit is not the motive and that other states, such as Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra, have similarly increased their bus fares.

The final fare adjustments are lower than the 45% increase recommended by the Public Transport Fare Regulatory Committee and fall well below the boards' initial requests of up to a 90% hike. While officials clarified that the free-ticket Shakti Scheme for eligible women is not responsible for the corporations' financial strain—noting that the government reimburses these costs despite occasional payment delays—the announcement itself was marked by confusion among reporters and officials regarding the exact percentage increases and fare structures.

Democrats Flip Key Senate Seats in Texas, Maine, Iowa, and South Carolina

In a series of major political upsets during the midterm elections, Democratic candidates flipped several key US Senate seats across the country, helping the party secure full control of the US Congress for the final two years of President Donald Trump's term. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico defeated Republican Ken Paxton, marking the first time a Democrat has won a Senate seat in the state since 1988. The race became the most expensive in US history, drawing massive financial backing, including $158 million from Elon Musk for a super PAC supporting Paxton.

In Maine, long-serving Republican moderate Susan Collins lost her seat to Democrat Troy Jackson, a former logger and state lawmaker. Jackson stepped into the race after the original Democratic nominee withdrew, campaigning heavily on workers' rights and tying Collins to President Trump. Meanwhile, South Carolina elected its first Democratic senator in nearly 28 years as former pediatrician Annie Andrews defeated Republican Darline Graham. Graham had been appointed to the seat following the sudden death of her brother, Senator Lindsey Graham, earlier in the year and faced criticism over her limited political and foreign policy experience.

Additional Democratic gains included victories in Iowa, where Josh Turek defeated Senator Ashley Hinson, and in Michigan, where Abdul El-Sayed defeated Mike Rogers to become the first Muslim American to serve in the Senate. These sweeping results contributed to a broader Democratic tidal wave that also unseated Texas Republican Governor Greg Abbott, reshaping the political landscape in several traditionally conservative or competitive states.

Canva Announces Major Restructure and Job Changes Affecting 22% of Workforce

Australian-founded design platform Canva has announced a major corporate restructure that will impact 1,271 workers, representing 22% of its global workforce. Co-founder and CEO Melanie Perkins described the announcement in an email to staff as the hardest moment in the company's 13-year history, noting that the changes will involve job cuts, role alterations, and relocations to centralized hubs. Employees affected by the cuts will receive at least six months of salary, continued healthcare coverage for 12 months where applicable, their laptops, and the option to apply for $10,000 grants to launch new ventures.

The restructuring comes after what the company described as its hardest year yet, marked by slower growth for the first time in a decade and decreased valuations from backers such as Blackbird Ventures and Airtree amid soaring expenses related to artificial intelligence technology. BBC News reported that the company's value dropped over concerns regarding the impact of AI and the cost of incorporating it into its platforms. Meanwhile, The Guardian noted that while Canva did not explicitly mention AI in its restructuring statement, the move reflects a broader trend across the technology sector where software firms are racing to stay competitive against AI disrupters while embedding AI tools into their own operations.

As part of the operational changes, Canva announced that Sydney will serve as the primary home for more of its product teams, and hybrid workers will be required to spend two to three days in the office starting next year. The company operates globally with staff across 37 countries.

The perpetrators then leveraged compromised computers and an internal AI tool to gain access to developer systems, ultimately executing a script to drain billions of dollars.

Kellogg's Reformulates UK Rice Krispies and Coco Pops Due to Weather Disruptions

Breakfast cereal giant Kellogg's has announced that it has removed rice grains from Rice Krispies and Coco Pops in the UK, replacing them with corn flour and rice flour. According to company spokespeople, the decision was driven by changing weather conditions in key Asian producing countries, where dry weather, droughts, floods, and heatwaves linked to the El Niño weather phenomenon have reduced harvests and driven up prices.

The updated recipes are designed to maintain consistent quality and availability as climate impacts affect global grain supplies. Kellogg's stated that the new formulations have been carefully assessed for taste, appearance, and texture, and that the products will continue to meet UK food standards regarding fat, salt, and sugar levels, while maintaining the same nutritional benefits such as fibre, Vitamin D, and iron.

The recipe changes introduce a significant allergen update for consumers in the UK. Because the new formulations use wheat flour or corn flour with added ingredients, the packaging will now include a "contains wheat gluten" warning, making the products unsuitable for shoppers with a wheat allergy or coeliac disease, whereas previously they only carried a "may contain gluten" cross-contamination warning. Meanwhile, Kellogg's North American business confirmed that Rice Krispies in the US and Canada will continue to be made with real rice with no recipe changes expected.

Jury Begins Deliberations in Russell Brand Trial

The jury at Southwark Crown Court in London has begun considering its verdicts in the trial of media personality Russell Brand. Brand, 51, has been on trial for three weeks, facing multiple charges including three counts of rape, three of sexual assault, and one count of attempted indecent assault, relating to allegations made by six women between 1999 and 2009. He has pleaded not guilty to all charges.

During the trial, prosecutors argued that Brand treated women as a "disposable commodity" and ignored them when they said no. Barrister Riel Karmy-Jones KC told the court that the complainants described how his personality could change from charming to frightening during the alleged incidents, and that they had been haunted by their experiences for years.

In his defense, Brand admitted to having been "obsessed" with sex and using his fame to facilitate sexual access, which he now views as wrong, but insisted that all his sexual encounters were consensual and denied any criminal conduct. His barrister contended that none of the encounters were unlawful and argued that allegations featured in a 2023 TV and newspaper investigation had negatively influenced the judicial process by prompting women to come forward.

Mr Justice Bennathan, the presiding judge, instructed the jury of eight men and four women that they must reach a unanimous verdict. The deliberations follow weeks of testimony detailing various allegations spanning a decade.
