Democrats Win Control of Both Chambers of Congress in Midterm Elections

In the recent US midterm elections, Democrats achieved a major victory by winning control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate, securing full control of Congress for the first time since 2023. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries celebrated the outcome on election night, while New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani urged fellow Democrats to fulfill their campaign promises following the blue wave.

Despite the legislative victory, Democrats face significant institutional checks. Republican President Donald Trump remains in office with the power to reject legislation, leading international figures like German Chancellor Friedrich Merz to suggest that the administration may continue to bypass Congress. Furthermore, Democratic figures like US Senator Chris Coons have cautioned against viewing the results as a broad mandate, noting that the party must still work to address public concerns regarding low wages and rising costs.

With their new majorities, congressional Democrats are positioned to utilize powerful oversight and investigative committees. Lawmakers have signaled potential inquiries into corporate corruption, government agencies, and the administration, alongside the Senate's authority to confirm or block judicial and cabinet nominations. However, any potential impeachment efforts would face a high barrier in the Senate, where a two-thirds majority is required for conviction.

  • Democrats won control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate in the midterm elections.
  • This marks the first time Democrats have held full control of Congress since 2023.
  • Republican President Donald Trump retains the power to veto or reject legislation passed by Congress.
  • Senate Democrats hold the power to confirm or block the president's judicial and cabinet nominations.
  • Foreign leaders and international observers are monitoring how the shift in congressional control will impact US foreign relations.

Hong Kong activist Joshua Wong sentenced to 10 years in prison under national security law

Prominent Hong Kong pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong has been sentenced to 10 years in prison after pleading guilty to colluding with foreign forces under the city's national security law. Wong, who turned 30 recently, had been accused of conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law to urge foreign governments to impose sanctions, blockades, or other hostile measures against China and Hong Kong authorities between July and late November 2020. High Court Judge William Tam described Wong's actions as grave, stating he served to supply underground support and draw foreign attention to bolster the cause.

Having already spent nearly six years behind bars following previous arrests related to his activism, Wong was due for release in January 2027 before receiving this latest sentence. While the judge used a 13-year starting point, he reduced it to 10 years due to Wong's guilty plea and mitigation, with two months and two days to run concurrently with his existing sentence. A packed public gallery, international diplomats, and heavy police presence attended the Thursday hearing.

The sentencing drew sharp international criticism and local defense of the legal system. Human rights organizations, the United States consulate, and Taiwan condemned the decision, describing it as political persecution and evidence that the national security law is used to punish dissent and silence advocates for fundamental freedoms. Conversely, Hong Kong Chief Executive John Lee and Beijing's foreign ministry defended the ruling, asserting that Wong and his co-conspirators undermined national sovereignty, security, and public well-being, and emphasizing that authorities remain committed to safeguarding national security in accordance with the law.

South Africa Holds Key Municipal Elections Amid Public Dissatisfaction

South Africans went to the polls on Wednesday for crucial municipal elections that represent a major electoral test for the ruling African National Congress (ANC). Voters cast ballots amid widespread discontent over deteriorating public infrastructure, persistent power cuts, and severe water outages that have affected major urban centers like Johannesburg.

While the ANC remains projected to be the largest party, polls indicated it could secure less than a third of the vote, continuing a downward trend from previous local and national elections. The resulting vote fragmentation means that unstable coalitions will likely dictate the governance of many of South Africa's 257 municipalities, continuing a pattern of high mayoral turnover that analysts associate with ongoing service delivery failures.

Opposition and alternative parties, including the centre-right Democratic Alliance (DA)—whose mayoral candidate Helen Zille campaigned actively in Johannesburg—alongside the leftwing uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) party and other political groups, are seeking to capitalize on voter frustration. Although the ANC's national leadership has shown openness to cooperating with parties like the DA, local party dynamics and the proliferation of coalition governments point toward a complex political landscape following the conclusion of the vote.

Cyclone Murasu Brings Heavy Rains, Flooding, and Rescue Operations to Telangana

Heavy rainfall triggered by Cyclone Murasu has caused widespread disruption, flooding, and agricultural damage across parts of Telangana, including the erstwhile undivided Khammam district. Mandals such as Aswaraopet and Dammapeta experienced severe waterlogging, inundated agricultural fields, and disrupted vehicular movement after a diversion road near Rajupeta was washed away. Local authorities responded by declaring school holidays in some affected areas and monitoring breaches in irrigation infrastructure, including a reported breach in the temporary ring bund of the Peddavagu medium irrigation project.

During rescue operations on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, two boats carrying ten personnel from the State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) and the Fire Department capsized in the Bethupalli Peddacheruvu lake in Khammam while attempting to rescue six stranded fishermen. Three personnel, including an SDRF member named Naresh, survived by clinging to trees amid raging floodwaters through the night until an NDRF team rescued them before dawn. All missing personnel were eventually accounted for after a strenuous search operation lasting more than 12 hours.

Officials attribute the sudden, dangerous water surges and strong currents that capsized the rescue boats to a suspected breach in a minor irrigation tank upstream. The incident underscored the hazards faced by emergency responders during severe weather events, prompting praise from officials such as Revenue Minister Ponguleti Srinivas Reddy, who lauded the coordinated efforts of the NDRF, SDRF, and other emergency response teams.

CVS Health Announces Plan to Split Into Two Public Companies by 2028

CVS Health announced plans on Wednesday to split into two separate publicly traded companies by the first half of 2028, effectively reversing its major 2018 acquisition of insurer Aetna and dismantling its vertically integrated healthcare conglomerate model. The planned breakup will separate the company's roughly 9,000 retail pharmacies from its major insurance business and pharmacy benefit manager, Caremark.

The insurance and PBM operations, along with units like Oak Street Health and Signify Health, will retain the CVS Health name and be led by CEO David Joyner. Meanwhile, the retail pharmacy business will form a separate company named CVS Pharmacy, to be led by current group president Prem Shah. Existing shareholders will own stock in both resulting companies.

Company executives described the move as coming from a position of strength, pointing to improved financial performance, a turnaround plan involving cost cuts and store closures, and eight consecutive quarters of beating Wall Street earnings estimates. However, despite posting strong third-quarter results that topped expectations, CVS shares dropped roughly 12% following the announcement, reflecting investor pessimism over the major corporate restructuring.

Binance Details Multi-Month Planning and Sophisticated Methods in Record Crypto Heist

The heist initially targeted $6.34 billion in cryptocurrency, marking the largest sum ever aimed at in a single cryptocurrency theft. Binance stated that it has successfully frozen or intercepted $2.85 billion of the stolen funds and has set aside the necessary capital to cover the remainder of the losses. Binance's findings indicate that the threat actors employed various sophisticated techniques, including fake recruitment interviews, malicious software downloads, compromised internal AI tooling, and endpoint compromise. Study warns warming UK seas could reshape marine ecosystems by 2050

A four-year study by an international team of researchers, led by the British government agency Cefas and published in Science Advances, has projected that warming seas around the UK and the wider north-east Atlantic could destabilize marine food webs, weaken ocean health, and jeopardize food security by 2050. The research challenges previous assumptions that marine biodiversity in middle latitudes like the UK would increase uniformly as species move northward from warmer regions.

The findings indicate that while groups higher up the food chain, such as fish and seabed invertebrates, may experience an increase in diversity of about 12% by mid-century under a scenario of 3C of warming by 2100, key plankton groups at the base of the food web are projected to decline. Specifically, phytoplankton and zooplankton diversity could decrease by about 3% and 7% respectively by 2050, creating a predator-prey imbalance.

Scientists highlighted that the southern North Sea and surrounding British waters are among 20 global "marine climate hotspots" that have warmed the fastest over the past 50 years. This uneven impact means certain fish species like anchovies, seabass, and sardines may act as climate winners, whereas species such as Atlantic cod, haddock, and coley face less suitable conditions.

The research underscores that warming does not uniformly alter biodiversity, surprising researchers who initially expected overall diversity to rise as southern species migrated northward. The resulting mismatch between rising predator diversity and falling prey diversity poses significant risks of disrupting marine food webs and the resilience of ocean ecosystems.
