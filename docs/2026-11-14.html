US Lifts Nationwide Ground Stop After Hours-Long Air Traffic Disruption

The US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) lifted a nationwide ground stop after a communications and radar disruption halted flights for more than four hours. The incident, which marked the longest nationwide ground stop since the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, caused significant travel disruption across the United States and internationally, impacting over 1.5 million passengers.

Trump administration officials attributed the outage to antiquated systems being overloaded by newly installed technology and equipment. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy stated there was no indication of a cyber-attack or foul play, emphasizing that flights in the air at the time were landed safely. However, officials noted that recovering from the stoppage would take several days due to displaced planes and crews.

Airlines and analytics firms reported thousands of flight delays and cancellations, alongside hundreds of diverted aircraft. Passengers faced long wait times and limited information at major airports, while various sports teams and international travel routes were also thrown into chaos. Officials expressed confidence that normal operations would be restored in time for the upcoming Thanksgiving travel rush.

US, Russian and Chinese Leaders Plan First-Ever Trilateral Meeting in China

The White House has announced that US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Chinese President Xi Jinping plan to hold an unprecedented joint meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit in Shenzhen, China. According to administration officials, the discussions will focus primarily on the reduction of global nuclear weapons and the US-Israeli conflict involving Iran, while discussions on Ukraine are reportedly not expected.

While the gathering brings together the leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers, American officials and outside analysts have noted that expectations for concrete outcomes remain low. A senior US official stated that no formal agreements are anticipated from the talks, describing the session as an opportunity for difficult conversations. The expiration of the US-Russia New START arms control treaty in February has left major arsenals without guardrails, and President Trump has repeatedly expressed a desire to replace it with a trilateral framework involving China.

However, Beijing has historically resisted joining such trilateral negotiations, arguing that the US and Russia, which hold vastly larger atomic stockpiles, should handle reductions directly. Chinese state media have noted bilateral meetings at the summit without confirming a trilateral format, and neither Beijing nor Moscow had officially commented on the announcement at the time of the White House statement. Analysts suggest the summit carries significant symbolic value, potentially allowing Beijing to showcase its global influence and offering Moscow a platform to project superpower status.

Under the new arrangements, an "eagle-eye" camera system modeled on airport technology will be tested at Eurotunnel terminals in Folkestone and Calais. This drive-through setup intends to handle passport and biometric checks without requiring travelers to exit their vehicles, averting potential border chaos and long tailbacks associated with upcoming entry and exit system (EES) regulations.

In addition to passenger transit, Environment Secretary Angela Eagle stated that a sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) deal—largely agreed upon earlier—will remove the vast majority of physical checks and paperwork for the plant and horticulture trade. While supporters emphasize that the agreements will lower business costs, reduce consumer prices, and ease trade friction, some businesses face sunk costs. For instance, certain nurseries built expensive private inspection facilities that will become obsolete once the new border rules take effect.
