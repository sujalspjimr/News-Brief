Severe Cyclone Arnab Crosses Andhra Coast and Weakens

Cyclone Arnab made landfall along the coasts of Andhra Pradesh and Yanam, bringing torrential rainfall, strong winds, and rough sea conditions before weakening from a very severe to a severe cyclonic storm, according to the India Meteorological Department (IMD). The severe weather caused widespread disruption, including waterlogging on major roads, uprooted trees, and surging sea waves that reached roads and residential areas in places such as Ennore in Chennai and Konaseema district.

Authorities launched extensive preparedness and rescue operations in response to the storm. Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu directed officials to swiftly clear fallen trees and restore power and traffic, while schools in 16 coastal districts were closed and 609 school campuses were converted into relief shelters. Over 17,000 volunteers, along with medical teams, ambulances, and police forces, were mobilized. Police notably rescued four people stranded by floodwaters in Kakinada, and thousands of vulnerable residents, including pregnant women, were moved to safety.

The severe weather also heavily impacted regional transportation, with at least 38 flights cancelled at Visakhapatnam airport, affecting domestic and international routes, while airlines like IndiGo warned of continued disruptions. Flash flood warnings were issued across multiple districts, and officials advised residents to stay indoors and avoid hazardous areas as heavy rains were expected to continue.

Delaware Proposal to Permit Autonomous AI-Run Businesses Draws Congressional Scrutiny

A proposed legislative initiative in Delaware aims to establish a new corporate entity known as an Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC), allowing autonomous AI agents to manage business affairs without a human at the helm. According to a draft bill, these entities could operate in nearly any lawful industry except banking, functioning within a 30-month regulatory sandbox. Proponents argue that the framework would generate state revenue and bring transparency to autonomous software by giving AI systems a recognizable legal identity, making their conduct visible, traceable, and accountable.

However, the proposal has faced pushback from federal lawmakers. U.S. Representative James Comer, chairman of the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, sent a letter to Delaware Governor Matt Meyer expressing concern that the initiative could allow human owners and investors to evade legal responsibility for reckless or harmful actions taken by AI. Comer also warned that the proposal could be exploited by non-transparent forces seeking anticompetitive regulatory capture, and indicated that the committee is probing the plan to inform potential federal legislation that might limit state AI regulatory authority.

Advocates of the legal framework maintain that attempting to prohibit autonomous AI activity is futile and that wrapping AI in legal forms creates a defined target for damages and liability. In contrast, critics contend that the initiative is ripe for abuse, potentially shielding powerful entities from accountability. Delaware remains a leading destination for business entities, and the state's administration previously argued that existing laws in several states do not clearly prevent AI agents from operating companies, necessitating proactive legal structures.

Key facts

  • A Delaware proposal seeks to create a new corporate entity called an Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC) managed entirely by AI agents.
  • The draft bill proposes a 30-month regulatory sandbox to test the concept, excluding only the banking industry.
  • U.S. Rep. James Comer sent a letter to Delaware Governor Matt Meyer requesting documents regarding the proposal and potential safety risks.
