Tamil Nadu Introduces 15-Day Paternity Leave for Government Employees

Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay has announced the introduction of 15 days of paternity leave for married male employees of the state government. According to official releases, the decision follows requests submitted by male government employees seeking the policy change.

Under the new guidelines, eligible employees can take the 15-day leave either starting 15 days before the expected date of childbirth or anytime within six months following the child's birth. The state government noted that the period surrounding childbirth is crucial for both the mother and the newborn, emphasizing that a father's involvement is essential for offering physical and emotional support.

This policy joins existing provisions in the state that grant female government employees 365 days of maternity leave. Officials stated that extending paternity leave aims to help fathers share family and childcare responsibilities and ensure greater paternal involvement in raising the child.

Supreme Court Quashes Remission of Anand Mohan in 2008 Murder Case

Mohan had previously been convicted and sentenced to life imprisonment for the crime before his release following a 2023 remission by the Bihar government.

The family of the slain officer, including his wife Uma Krishnaiah and daughter Padma, welcomed the Supreme Court's ruling. While expressing relief and satisfaction with the judicial outcome, the family noted that the decision could not reverse their irreparable loss. Padma stated that their legal challenge specifically opposed the alteration of jail rules that facilitated the remission.

The verdict drew political reactions in Bihar. The opposition Rashtriya Janata Dal criticized the ruling coalition of the Bharatiya Janata Party and Janata Dal (United), accusing them of adjusting jail regulations for political convenience. In response, a BJP spokesperson dismissed the allegations as baseless claims from a frustrated opposition, while a JD(U) representative declined to comment on the court's decision.

FCC Approves SpaceX Plan for Million-Satellite Constellation

The US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has granted SpaceX conditional authorization to deploy a constellation of up to one million satellites known as the "Starmind" initiative. The project aims to place artificial intelligence chip-equipped data centers into low-Earth orbit, utilizing sunlight to power computing tasks and transmitting results back to Earth via high-speed lasers linked with the existing Starlink network. Regulators describe the approval as a bold step to support America's super intelligence economy and maintain technological leadership.

The unprecedented regulatory approval provides SpaceX with a long timeline for deployment. The company must launch its first Starmind satellite by November 2033, deploy 10 percent (or 100,000 satellites) by November 2035, and reach the full one-million-satellite milestone by November 2040. However, the FCC deferred parts of SpaceX's initial proposal, such as its request for waivers allowing retired satellites above 600km to be moved into graveyard or heliocentric orbits rather than burning up in the atmosphere, citing insufficient information.

The decision has drawn mixed reactions from competitors and scientific communities. Proponents argue that orbital data centers offer a much-needed, energy-efficient alternative to terrestrial facilities amid growing domestic opposition to terrestrial data center expansion. Conversely, critics including rival firm Amazon have questioned the feasibility of the plan, while US astronomers caution that such a massive constellation—which would increase current orbital satellite counts roughly 50-fold—will significantly undermine the public's ability to observe the night sky.

The company detected the intrusion on November 19 after an attacker compromised an employee's corporate GitHub account. Using this access, the hacker pushed malicious code to backend services and internal servers to download customer data, including names, login email addresses, public keys, account and user identifiers, and metadata regarding account creation and last login times. Tokens from certain emailed invitation or recovery links were also accessed.

Despite the breach of backend systems, 1Password stated that customer password vaults and other stored secrets were not compromised and were "at no point" available to the attacker. The company noted that it does not have access to customer account passwords or secret keys, and its apps and extensions remain unaffected. However, the exposure of names, emails, and tokens creates potential risks for users, particularly regarding targeted phishing attempts that may reference account invitations or recovery efforts.

In response to the incident, 1Password disabled the affected GitHub account, rotated compromised internal credentials, and implemented a new detection system to guard against similar attacks. The company also increased the length of codes sent to users for account recovery to make them harder to guess and advised customers to remain vigilant against phishing. At the time of disclosure, it remained unclear how many users were affected or how the initial access to the employee's account was achieved.
