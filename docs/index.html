Russia and Ukraine Hold US-Mediated Talks in Abu Dhabi

Negotiators from Russia and Ukraine met in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, for U.S.-mediated negotiations aimed at ending the war. The discussions, which included envoys Kushner and Witkoff representing the administration of President Trump, lasted nearly four hours on their first day and extended into a second day. During the opening round, Washington urged the establishment of a "new" security framework.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy expressed optimism regarding the diplomatic engagement, describing the initial talks as "constructive and quite substantive." Zelenskyy also stated that he was "happy" with the shifts in approach adopted by U.S. mediators. Despite these developments, expectations remain tempered given that three previous rounds of negotiations early this year failed to produce significant breakthroughs.

The diplomatic efforts coincided with widespread military strikes exchanged between both nations. Ukrainian drone attacks targeted Russia’s northwest, causing a fire at the Ust-Luga port area and striking refinery facilities, while Russian strikes damaged electrical substations and infrastructure across Ukrainian cities such as Kryvyi Rih, Kharkiv, and Zaporizhzhia. The concurrent attacks also prompted drone alerts in Baltic nations and temporary airspace restrictions in Finland.

Israel Swears In New Parliament Amid Tense Standoff Over Coalition Formation

Israel's new parliament was sworn in on Tuesday during a tense opening session marked by fierce political exchanges between caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot. Following October's general election, in which almost half of the incoming lawmakers did not serve in the previous parliament, neither bloc secured an outright majority in the 120-seat Knesset. President Isaac Herzog officially tasked Eisenkot, leader of the centrist Yashar party and a former army chief, with forming the next government. Eisenkot's four-party bloc holds 48 seats against 53 for Netanyahu's bloc, granting Eisenkot 28 days—with a possible two-week extension—to assemble a governing coalition.

During the swearing-in proceedings, Netanyahu warned lawmakers against entering any coalition supported by Arab-led parties, describing those factions as "supporters of terror and haters of Israel" seeking the elimination of the state. He warned of the threat of civil war while calling for a broad national unity government under his own leadership to counter threats such as a "nuclear Iran." Netanyahu's proposed unity formula would include his existing partners, including the far-right politicians Bezalel Smotrich and Itamar Ben-Gvir. At the ceremony, Netanyahu was also repeatedly heckled by former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett.

Eisenkot strongly rebuked Netanyahu's address, describing it as "delusional, divisive, hate-filled and shameful" and arguing that the caretaker premier is unfit for office. Eisenkot reiterated his call for a "national emergency government" that asks parties to put aside ideological differences, requiring adherence to principles that include recognizing Israel as a Jewish and democratic state. He also pledged to open an official inquiry into Hamas's October 7, 2023 attacks and to reverse many of Netanyahu's policies.

Government negotiations remain complex, with Arab-led alliances—the United Arab List and the Joint List—holding an unprecedented combined 19 seats. While Netanyahu has ruled out working with Arab parties and several opposition factions also resist partnering with them, focus has turned to the ultra-Orthodox faction United Torah Judaism (UTJ). UTJ declined to endorse Netanyahu and is reportedly holding talks to back Eisenkot, an alignment that analysts suggest could potentially allow Eisenkot to secure a 67-seat majority.

  • President Isaac Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming a government within 28 days, plus a potential 14-day extension.
  • Arab-led parties won an unprecedented combined 19 seats, but face opposition from both Netanyahu and members of the opposition bloc.
  • Eisenkot pledged to establish an official state inquiry into the October 7, 2023 attacks and called for a national emergency government.

Ebola Outbreak in Democratic Republic of the Congo Exceeds 11,000 Cases

Government data released in November 2026 shows that confirmed cases of Ebola in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) have reached 11,042, with at least 5,387 deaths. This marks the largest and deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country's history and the second-largest on record globally, trailing only the 2014-2016 West African epidemic.

Health authorities have faced significant challenges in containing the virus, which is caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain. Factors such as delayed detection, ongoing conflict, and strained health services have contributed to the rapid spread across eight of the DRC's 26 provinces, with numerous deaths occurring outside of treatment centers.

The situation is further complicated by the fact that no vaccine or treatment has been developed specifically for the Bundibugyo strain, which kills approximately half of those it infects. In response to the widening geographic spread across the continent, the World Health Organization approved clinical trials for two new Ebola vaccines earlier in the month, with results expected in early 2027.

US House Defeats Republican-Led Bid to Impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth

The US House of Representatives has voted to block an impeachment resolution targeting Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Introduced by dissident Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, the articles of impeachment accused Hegseth of waging a war in Iran for more than 90 days without congressional authorization, alongside overseeing extrajudicial killings during naval counter-narcotics campaigns and operations in Venezuela and Yemen.

The House voted 211 to 204 to refer the measure to the Republican-led House Judiciary Committee, a procedural move that effectively kills the current effort given the short time remaining before the new Congress convenes in January. Representative Massie was the sole Republican to vote against the referral, while over a dozen members missed the vote, including 11 Democrats. Massie acknowledged he was disappointed by the outcome, comparing his colleagues to passengers staying on a sinking ship.

President Donald Trump celebrated the vote on Truth Social, describing Hegseth as a "Great American Patriot" and pointing to military recruitment numbers and recent operations as validation of his leadership. Defense Secretary Hegseth fiercely dismissed the allegations during a Pentagon briefing, calling the impeachment push a "farce," "sabotage," and a political stunt. Although Hegseth survived this immediate legislative challenge, analysts note he could face renewed scrutiny or a fresh impeachment push when Democrats take control of both congressional chambers in January.

Mother Dairy Hikes Milk Prices by Rs 2-4 Per Litre

Mother Dairy has announced an increase in milk prices by Rs 2 to Rs 4 per litre across various liquid milk variants in the Delhi-NCR region, effective November 12. Under the revision, full cream milk (pouched) rises from Rs 72 to Rs 76 per litre, toned milk goes from Rs 60 to Rs 62, double toned milk from Rs 54 to Rs 56, and cow milk from Rs 62 to Rs 65 per litre. Additionally, bulk-vended toned milk has increased from Rs 58 to Rs 60 per litre.

According to sources, the price adjustment is driven by rising input costs, specifically an increase in procurement prices paid to farmers. The company states that it passes on approximately 75 to 80 percent of its milk sales realization back to farmers. The spokesperson for Mother Dairy declined to comment on the matter.

The price hike contributes to household expenses at a time when food inflation remains elevated. National Statistics Office data indicates that food inflation rose to 7.74 percent in September, up from 5.97 percent in August. This marks Mother Dairy's latest price revision following previous adjustments in May 2026 and April 2025. Mother Dairy is a wholly owned subsidiary of the National Dairy Development Board and sells over 50 lakh litres of milk daily, with roughly 35 lakh litres sold in the Delhi-NCR market.

Federal judge allows Trump's $10bn defamation lawsuit against BBC to proceed

A federal judge in Florida has ruled that US President Donald Trump's $10bn defamation lawsuit against the BBC can move forward, denying a motion by the British broadcaster to dismiss the case. The legal action stems from an episode of the BBC's Panorama documentary, titled Trump: A Second Chance, which spliced together two separate segments of a speech Trump gave nearly 55 minutes apart ahead of the 6 January 2021 riot at the US Capitol. The BBC previously apologized, acknowledging the edit gave the mistaken impression that Trump had made a direct call for violent action.

In his ruling, Judge Jeffrey Kuntz found that Trump had plausibly alleged damages and that the broadcaster's editing decisions could support an inference of actual malice, which is required for a public figure to win a defamation claim. However, the judge dismissed a secondary claim in the lawsuit that accused the BBC of violating Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act, ruling that the documentary did not constitute commercial speech.

Trump celebrated the decision on social media as a "huge victory." Meanwhile, a BBC spokesperson stated that the organization was surprised by the ruling but respected the court's authority, adding that the network remains confident in its position as the case advances to the next stage. Whether Trump can ultimately prove his claims and establish damages at trial remains to be determined.

Kennedys Sue to Block Trump Arch Over Concerns Regarding JFK Gravesite

Members of the Kennedy family have joined a lawsuit seeking to block the construction of a 250-foot triumphal arch planned by President Donald Trump near Arlington National Cemetery. Nine members of the political dynasty argue that the structure, often referred to as the Arc de Trump, would cast shadows and block historically significant sightlines to the grave of former President John F. Kennedy and the graves of thousands of veterans buried at the cemetery.

The legal challenge contends that the monument would permanently alter the visitor experience and constitute a permanent denigration of the gravesites, disrupting the view from the eternal flame memorializing the former president. The complaint also asserts that the National Park Service and the Interior Department authorized construction without necessary congressional approval. The plaintiffs joining the litigation include several children and grandchildren of Robert, Ethel, and Ted Kennedy, though the filing does not include Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a member of Trump's cabinet.

In response, the White House criticized the legal action as an unfortunate politicization of a structure intended to honor veterans resting at Arlington. A White House spokesperson defended the project as a fitting monument designed to unite the nation for the country's 250th anniversary and expressed confidence that the administration would be vindicated in court. The project had recently received approval from the National Capital Planning Commission before a federal judge temporarily halted construction to allow ongoing litigation to proceed.
