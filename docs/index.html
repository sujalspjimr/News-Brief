Binance Resumes Withdrawals Following Record $6.3 Billion Cyberattack

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance has begun reopening and allowing customer withdrawals following a record-breaking $6.3 billion security breach that occurred on Thursday. The platform temporarily suspended all transactions in the immediate wake of the incident, which impacted digital assets across its global centralised exchange. As operations resumed in phases on Sunday, Binance reported processing a net outflow of roughly $3.64 billion in customer withdrawals as activity spiked before beginning to stabilise.

Major organisations, including the FBI, have attributed the massive cyber-attack to a North Korean state-backed group known as TraderTraitor or the Lazarus Group. According to investigators, the sophisticated attack involved a compromised engineer's laptop and malicious software featuring a self-destruct mechanism, and the stolen funds are actively being laundered across thousands of wallet addresses. North Korea has consistently denied involvement in such cyberattacks, and US President Donald Trump noted that the US administration was still verifying the identity of the culprits.

In response to the heist, Binance has pledged to absorb the financial impact so that users do not bear the losses. The company stated it has covered between 90% and 95% of the stolen assets using a combination of its corporate treasury, futures insurance, and emergency reserve funds, while noting that sourcing the remaining less-liquid tokens may take slightly longer.

  • Binance suffered a security breach impacting $6.3 billion in digital assets, marking the largest cryptocurrency hack in history.
  • The FBI attributed the cyberattack to the North Korean state-backed group TraderTraitor (also known as the Lazarus Group).
  • Binance reported covering between 90% and 95% of the stolen funds using its corporate treasury and emergency reserves.
  • Customer withdrawals resumed in phases, resulting in a net outflow of approximately $3.64 billion as trading reopened.
  • Investigators from Chainalysis and law enforcement observed stolen funds being actively layered and laundered across thousands of addresses.

Small Jet Crashes in Florida Street, Killing Nine

A business jet crashed into a residential and commercial area in Palm Beach County, Florida, on Sunday, resulting in the deaths of all nine people on board. The Learjet 60, which had departed from Austin, Texas, and was headed to the Bahamas, nosedived into a multi-lane road near Cresthaven, creating a large crater and erupting into a massive fireball. No injuries or deaths were reported among people on the ground.

According to air traffic control recordings and flight tracking data, the crew reported encountering moderate turbulence, icing, and precipitation while flying at 35,000 feet. The situation escalated rapidly as the aircraft entered a steep and uncontrolled descent, plummeting initially at 10,000 feet per minute and later accelerating past design limits to rates exceeding 20,000 feet per minute. The final transmission from the cockpit indicated an overspeed condition and included the words 'we're out of time' shortly before impact.

In response to the disaster, local authorities evacuated approximately 600 residents from a nearby trailer-park community and established emergency shelters. The impact severely damaged local infrastructure, including water lines and electrical service, and officials stated that the affected roadway would remain closed for at least 30 days. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) have launched an official investigation to determine the exact cause of the crash, while the identities of those on board have not yet been publicly released.

Foreign Nationals Among 69 Dead in Spanish High-Speed Train Collision

Authorities have confirmed that citizens from the United States, Japan, Canada, Argentina, Mexico, Tunisia, the Czech Republic, France, and Portugal are among the 69 people killed in a high-speed train collision in central Spain. The disaster occurred when a state-operated Renfe train struck the rear of a stationary train operated by the private company Iryo near Urda, south of Madrid. Forensic teams and police have been working to identify the victims, leaving families waiting as identifications are confirmed.

The tragedy has drawn international attention, including tributes to notable victims such as Dr Marta Shahbazi, an award-winning University of Cambridge researcher and mother of two who was traveling to collect a scientific prize. The accident is noted as one of Europe's deadliest rail disasters this century and Spain's worst since 2013, prompting three days of national mourning.

Investigations have focused on how the crash occurred after an automatic safety system failure halted trains on the track, prior to a control centre authorizing the Renfe service to resume its journey while another train remained stationary. The incident has also raised broader questions regarding the country's rail safety, following an earlier collision in January in Andalusia, and comes ahead of a general election in which Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez seeks to extend his premiership.
