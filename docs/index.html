Americans Head to Polls in High-Stakes Midterm Elections

Americans are voting in pivotal midterm elections to determine control of both chambers of Congress, with all 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 of the 100 Senate seats up for grabs. The elections serve as a crucial referendum on President Donald Trump's second term, coming amid public concern over high inflation, the cost of living, and an ongoing war with Iran that has driven up fuel prices.

The elections also test the boundaries of presidential power and institutional checks and balances. While Republicans currently hold narrow majorities in Congress, Democrats are aiming to capture majorities to strengthen legislative oversight on administration policies, immigration enforcement, and federal spending. Meanwhile, the administration's actions regarding election procedures have faced significant judicial resistance, with numerous federal court decisions blocking certain initiatives.

Polls begin closing on Tuesday evening starting on the East Coast at 6 pm EST, though close races and processing methods in certain states mean final outcomes may take days to be officially determined. President Trump has campaigned heavily across the country, though facing record low approval ratings, while attempting to lower expectations by asserting that sitting presidents typically do not win midterm elections.

  • Voters are deciding all 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 of the 100 Senate seats.
  • The cost of living, inflation, and high fuel prices driven in part by tariffs and the war with Iran are top concerns for voters.
  • Control of Congress and the legislative check on President Donald Trump's administration are at stake.
  • The first polling stations close on Tuesday at 6 pm EST in parts of Indiana and Kentucky.

Russian Airstrikes Kill Dozens at Humanitarian Aid Center in Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia Region

At least 26 to 29 people were killed when Russian aerial bombs struck a humanitarian aid distribution center and neighboring shelter in the village of Komyshuvakha, located in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region. Ukrainian officials reported that the attack involved multiple strikes, including a second bombing targeting people who had gathered or taken shelter after the initial impact. The dead included rescue workers and police officers, and numerous individuals remained missing or injured under the rubble.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy condemned the bombing as a deliberate terrorist strike, noting that residents regularly gathered at the location every Monday for humanitarian aid and that Russian forces could not have been unaware of its use. Regional officials added that rescue operations were temporarily hindered by subsequent Russian drone attacks in the area. A day of mourning was declared across the Zaporizhzhia region.

The attack occurred shortly after a temporary humanitarian ceasefire ended in nearby areas where civilians had been evacuated. Ukrainian leaders suggested the strikes undermine hopes for de-escalation following recent temporary truces. Meanwhile, US special envoy Steve Witkoff announced that teams from Ukraine, Russia, and the US are scheduled to resume peace discussions in Abu Dhabi, though expectations for a major breakthrough remain low as the war continues into its fifth year.

US officials stated that the strikes were a direct response to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) firing upon a US aircraft.

The military action comes amid an ongoing conflict that began when the US and Israel attacked Iran in February. President Donald Trump, facing falling polling numbers and pressure from his party to end the war, stated on Monday that he was in "no rush" to ramp up strikes and noted that Iran had been crippled financially. Meanwhile, regional diplomats are intensifying mediation efforts. Qatar has led intense talks over a 72-hour period to prevent further US attacks, and Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi reported that intensive negotiations to end the war are underway with the US via mediators.

Political analysts and experts note that the outcome of the US midterms could alter the political landscape for the remainder of Trump's term, with a potential Democratic victory likely to increase domestic scrutiny on the war's financial costs. However, experts point out that the president retains broad authority over foreign policy and military operations, meaning limited strikes could continue regardless of congressional shifts. At the same time, regional political dynamics—including recent political deadlock following Israel's elections and ongoing talks involving Qatar and Saudi Arabia—continue to influence the fragile security situation in the Gulf.

North Korea denies involvement in record $6.3bn Binance hack

North Korea has formally denied allegations by the United States that it was behind the record $6.3 billion cyberattack on cryptocurrency exchange Binance. Kim Yo Jong, a senior official and sister of leader Kim Jong Un, dismissed the accusations as "absurd rhetoric" and a "grave political provocation" intended to damage Pyongyang's public image. The denial follows statements from the FBI, the Secret Service, and blockchain analysts attributing the massive theft to the state-backed hacking group TraderTraitor, also known as the Lazarus Group.

The breach, which unfolded over a span of about twenty minutes, stands as the largest cryptocurrency exchange heist in history, eclipsing previous major incidents such as the 2025 Bybit raid. According to preliminary Binance investigations, the attack was facilitated by a compromised engineer's laptop that enabled unauthorized withdrawal transactions. In response to the crisis, Binance worked with token issuers and used its own funds to replace the lost amounts, subsequently resuming major trading and withdrawals as security measures were restored.

Analysts suggest that North Korea's prompt and forceful denial stems from concerns that Washington will leverage the incident to further restrict funding streams allegedly used to bankroll the country's sanctioned economy and arms development. Meanwhile, Pyongyang has warned that ongoing hostility from the United States could negatively impact future diplomatic engagements and broader geopolitical relations.

Supreme Court Addresses Delhi Protest Sites and Firecracker Sales Restrictions

The Supreme Court of India addressed two separate legal matters involving the National Capital Region, issuing remarks on the duration of protest demonstrations and raising concerns over the alleged open sale of uncertified firecrackers.

During a hearing on petitions filed by traders' associations regarding the economic and operational impact of demonstrations against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, Chief Justice of India Surya Kant stated that protest sites cannot be occupied indefinitely. The bench emphasized that the right to protest must be balanced with the rights of other citizens and that designated venues should be rotated for specific periods. The court also noted that permission orders must clearly state reasons for approval or refusal, and it issued notices to the Centre and authorities while asking traders to implead the protest organizers.

In a separate proceeding, a bench comprising Justices MM Sundresh and Prasanna B Varale flagged reports highlighting the continued open availability of conventional and uncertified firecrackers in Delhi markets. This occurred despite a recent court order permitting only certified green crackers ahead of Diwali with specific time restrictions, bans on barium and cracker strings, and requirements for product-specific QR codes. The bench directed Additional Solicitor General Aishwarya Bhati to examine the reported violations.

US Midterm Campaign Shatters Spending Records to Become Most Expensive Ever

The 2026 United States midterm election campaign has surpassed previous financial records, with total costs projected by the independent campaign finance organization Open Secrets to reach nearly $12 billion (£9 billion). This figure represents roughly a 30% increase compared to the spending seen during the 2022 midterm elections.

A significant portion of this financial activity is being driven by outside political groups, specifically Super Political Action Committees (Super PACs), which are permitted to accept unlimited contributions from individuals, corporations, and unions to indirectly support candidates. According to Federal Election Commission filings up to mid-October, total spending had already exceeded $9.5 billion, with PACs and Super PACs accounting for more than half of that amount.

The influx of funding features substantial contributions from wealthy individuals and entities on both sides of the political spectrum. Billionaire businessman Elon Musk is reported as the top individual donor, having spent nearly half a billion dollars primarily through the America PAC to back Republican candidates. Other notable contributors to conservative causes include Jeff Yass and Richard and Elizabeth Uihlein. On the Democratic side, organizations founded by billionaire financier George Soros have contributed significantly, and former New York City mayor Michael Bloomberg has given more than $60 million to liberal groups.

Investigators from the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) are examining the circumstances surrounding a private jet crash in Florida that resulted in the deaths of all nine people on board. The Learjet 60, flying from Austin, Texas, to the Bahamas, nosedived and exploded on a multi-lane highway street on Sunday, located nine miles from President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago resort. In addition to those killed on the aircraft, one person on the ground sustained a leg injury.

Flight data and air traffic control audio indicate that the aircraft's crew reported encountering ice and moderate turbulence shortly after takeoff, requesting a higher altitude. Less than 30 seconds later, the plane began a rapid descent, with the crew issuing a mayday call and reporting an extreme descent rate before final transmissions became unintelligible. NTSB officials stated they are investigating whether parts of the aircraft broke off before the crash, alongside potential contributions from ice, turbulence, and human error.

The victims on board have been identified through family statements and media reports as including tech entrepreneur Adam Robinson, his wife Helen Sharp, their two young children Emma and Charlie, along with passengers Rachael Reed, Charmi Fehr, and Anita Maria Cetina. The flight crew consisted of pilot Jeff Gregory and 20-year-old copilot Nathalia Filgueiras. Officials noted that recovering the wreckage from the impact crater and locating the cockpit voice recorder will present a challenging and lengthy investigatory process involving multiple agencies.

The UK government has launched its first major study to examine the levels of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), commonly known as "forever chemicals," within the UK population. Led by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA), the research will involve analyzing blood and urine samples gathered through the Health Survey for England. Alongside PFAS, the study will also look for other potential contaminants such as lead, arsenic, bisphenols, phthalates, and flame retardants.

PFAS have been widely utilized since the 1940s and 1950s due to their resistance to water, oil, and heat, making them common components in items like cookware, clothing, and firefighting foams. However, because they do not break down easily in the environment, they have attracted increasing health concerns. While current evidence suggests limited health impacts from PFAS overall, large population groups have shown strong links between the substances and raised cholesterol as well as lower birth weights. Additionally, prolonged exposure to two specific types already banned in the UK—PFOA and PFOS—can increase cancer risks according to the World Health Organization.

The initiative comes amid growing public anxiety, particularly among residents living near contamination sites who have sought individual blood tests. Despite finding high levels of PFOA near a major chemical factory in September, the UK government currently advises against routine individual testing. Officials explain that there is no established clinical intervention available for individuals found to have elevated levels, meaning a test with no actionable follow-up is generally not recommended. Experts note that low levels of PFAS are likely present in everyone's blood, and the new population study aims to build a broader evidence base to better understand the long-term health implications of different exposure levels, with initial results expected next year.
