Powerful Magnitude 7.0 Earthquake Hits Tibet, Causing Casualties and Destruction

A powerful magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck a mountainous, high-altitude region in Tibet on Thursday night, resulting in at least 16 deaths and numerous injuries. According to state media and official reports, the epicenter hit Baqên County in the Nagqu region, causing widespread destruction including the collapse of hundreds of homes and livestock sheds, downed power and communication lines, and blocked roads due to rockfalls.

In response to the disaster, Chinese authorities launched emergency rescue operations, deploying thousands of personnel—including police, firefighters, and local workers—to the affected areas. Relief supplies such as tents, prefabricated housing units, and winter clothing were dispatched, and the government allocated millions for disaster relief. Top leaders, including President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang, called for all-out efforts in rescue and medical treatment, while local residents braved subzero temperatures outdoors overnight amid hundreds of aftershocks.

The thin sources available outline the immediate logistical challenges posed by the high altitude of around 4,800 meters, subzero conditions, and expected intermittent snow over subsequent days. The disaster highlights the seismic vulnerability of the region, which has experienced previous major earthquakes and natural disasters in recent years.

SpaceX and Tesla Consider Potential Mega-Merger

Elon Musk is considering a potential mega-merger between his two major companies, Tesla and electric vehicle and rocket manufacturer SpaceX. Tesla is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion, while SpaceX is valued at $2 trillion, meaning a combination of the two firms would represent the largest corporate merger in history.

Musk stated that special committees have been established to assess a potential deal, though he noted that nothing has been agreed upon and there is no guarantee a merger will ultimately take place. Any potential agreement would require approval from Tesla shareholders, with half of the needed votes required to back the deal—a threshold Musk described as a high hurdle. Because he leads both companies, Musk cannot participate in shareholder discussions regarding the transaction.

Proponents of the deal argue that the two companies share complementary technologies in areas such as artificial intelligence, chips, robotics, and data centers. Combining Tesla's vehicles and autonomous driving tech with SpaceX's rockets and satellite telecommunications could also advance goals like building a self-sustaining city on Mars. Conversely, critics and financial analysts note that SpaceX carries high credit risk due to heavy borrowing for hardware like Nvidia chips, and the merger would leave Tesla shareholders holding a smaller percentage of a much larger and more complex enterprise with no clear valuation precedent.

Global Cyber-Security Scramble After Critical PHP Flaw Discovered

Organisations and businesses worldwide are scrambling to update their systems following the discovery of critical vulnerabilities in PHP, a widely-used back-end programming language that powers an estimated 70% of the world's websites. Websites across the globe were taken offline on Wednesday and Thursday after developers and security researchers revealed severe inherent flaws—coined "php2shell"—affecting versions released over the last decade. The vulnerabilities were notably uncovered using AI technology, with firms like Wiz describing the issues as the most severe detected by artificial intelligence, and OpenAI calling it one of the most impactful security issues in recent memory.

Governments and cyber-security agencies responded by urging operators to install emergency fixes or temporarily take platforms offline as a precaution. For instance, the UK government took a number of limited services offline, with tech minister Baroness Lloyd of Effra noting that 32 government services were unavailable and four partially unavailable. While cybersecurity companies reported millions of attempts to exploit the flaw globally, officials and experts emphasized that there have been no confirmed successful breaches or attacks so far, though the full impact may take months to become apparent.

The situation has sparked discussions regarding infrastructure upkeep and the role of artificial intelligence in cyber security. Cybersecurity experts noted that AI could make attacks easier and faster, yet it was also the tool that successfully brought the long-hidden, decade-old programming flaws to light. Authorities continue to work with international partners and technology firms to apply mitigations and secure web servers against potential exploitation.

Moderna and Merck Release Positive Phase Data for mRNA Cancer Vaccine

Pharmaceutical partners Moderna and Merck have released detailed late-stage clinical trial data for their personalized mRNA cancer vaccine, known as intismeran autogene. The trial evaluated the vaccine in combination with Merck's immunotherapy drug Keytruda for patients with surgically removed melanoma, the deadliest form of skin cancer. According to the findings, the combination treatment reduced the risk of cancer recurrence or death by 42% compared to Keytruda alone, and lowered the risk of the cancer spreading or death by 38%. Additionally, about 82% of patients receiving the combination were alive without their melanoma returning at two years, compared to 70% for the control group.

The results were made public following an early data leak from an oncology news site, prompting the companies to release the full top-line results ahead of their scheduled presentation at the European Society of Medical Oncology conference and publication in the New England Journal of Medicine. Despite the positive clinical findings, shares of Moderna dropped significantly following the leak, a market reaction analysts attributed to inflated investor expectations prior to the release rather than concerns over the therapy's efficacy.

The trial's success has bolstered confidence among researchers, oncologists, and executives regarding the potential of personalized mRNA technology in oncology. Moderna and Merck are already engaging with regulatory authorities regarding approval and are studying the vaccine in clinical trials across other tumor types, such as non-small cell lung cancer, bladder cancer, and renal cell carcinoma. However, company leaders and experts note that further research is required to demonstrate whether the treatment can be effective across a broader range of cancers and whether it ultimately extends overall patient survival.

While state law could have warranted a penalty of over $219 billion, and New Mexico's legal team had asked for between $35 billion and $40 billion, Judge Mathew reduced the final figure to $25 billion. State officials argued that penalties needed to be severe enough to impact Meta's stock price and force meaningful changes, noting that prior penalties like a $5 billion settlement with the Federal Trade Commission had not deterred deceptive trade practices. Meta had argued that anything more than $3.4 billion would be unconstitutional and hoped penalties would align more closely with past settlements.

The massive financial penalty arrives as Meta faces a wave of lawsuits related to user safety and privacy. The company reported significant financial impacts from legal settlements in its quarterly earnings report, including a separate $9.9 billion settlement payment in the US and an agreement to pay $17 billion to a coalition of state attorneys general regarding child safety. Meta plans to appeal the New Mexico decision, and a spokesperson did not immediately respond to a request for comment following the judge's order.

According to the founders, the startup's primary goal is to rebuild the domestic supply of working dogs and reduce the United States' reliance on European breeders. The company states that over 80 percent of the U.S. military's working dogs currently come from Europe, and less than half ultimately prove fit for service. Warhound has collected blood and cell samples from top-performing military, police, and rescue dogs in the U.S. and plans to use gene-editing technology to eliminate common health conditions, such as hip dysplasia, while potentially enhancing traits like smell, speed, and agility. The first puppies are anticipated to be born in early 2027.

In addition to genetic modifications, Warhound plans to equip the canines with hardware and software developed by Anduril, such as advanced body armor, protective vests, and situational awareness tools. Lamm clarified that these enhancements are worn on the animal rather than implanted inside the body. The startup intends to operate under the guidance of the American Humane Society, focusing on improving canine health, performance, and safety alongside human handlers in dangerous operations.
