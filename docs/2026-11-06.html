Democrats Win Control of Congress in Midterm Elections and Plan Investigations

Following the 2026 midterm elections, Democrats have won control of both chambers of Congress in a sweeping victory that surpassed many forecasters' expectations, including flipping at least seven Republican-held Senate seats and multiple congressional seats in states like North Carolina. While some individual House and Senate races remain too close to call and are still undergoing vote counts—such as tight contests in Alaska and Mississippi—the broader outcome gives Democrats congressional majorities.

With these new majorities, Democrats have gained full investigatory powers, including the ability to issue subpoenas and compel testimony, and have outlined plans to investigate the Trump administration. In addition to conducting oversight, Democrats campaigned heavily on the issue of affordability, responding to voter concerns over high grocery, electricity, and fuel costs despite a healthy macroeconomic picture on paper. Their legislative and policy priorities include bringing back extra financial help for health insurance under the Affordable Care Act, reversing previous Medicaid cuts, eliminating certain import tariffs, addressing energy prices, building four million new homes, and increasing taxes on billionaires alongside family tax credits.

Despite capturing Congress, analysts note that the path forward involves significant legislative hurdles. President Donald Trump retains executive veto power, leading observers to suggest that cooperation or stalemate may define the upcoming political landscape as both parties navigate divided governance.

Federal Judge Temporarily Blocks Construction of Trump's Triumphal Arch

A federal judge has temporarily halted construction of President Donald Trump's planned 250-foot triumphal arch in Washington, issuing a 14-day restraining order just after midnight following an approval vote by a federal panel. The National Capital Planning Commission (NCPC) had voted 8-4 in favor of the project late Thursday evening following hours of public comment and opposition. The temporary order by US District Judge Tanya Chutkan pauses any immediate work while lawsuits challenging the project proceed.

The proposed monument, often compared to the Arc de Triomphe in Paris and intended to commemorate the country's 250th anniversary, has drawn sharp divisions. Trump-appointed commissioners and administration supporters argued that the arch will serve as a grand tribute to US history and veterans, fulfilling long-standing plans for Columbia Island while providing Arlington National Cemetery with a monumental gateway. The administration also noted the structure would feature military equipment storage capabilities, including deployment systems for snipers and drones.

Conversely, opponents including veterans, local residents, and officials from Washington and Virginia have strongly criticized the project. Critics argue that the towering structure would obstruct views of and diminish the solemnity surrounding Arlington National Cemetery. The legal challenges involve consolidated lawsuits from the District of Columbia, Virginia, Vietnam veterans, and an architectural historian, reflecting broader tensions surrounding the administration's efforts to reshape the capital city.

California Governor Commutes Sentences of All Death Row Inmates

California Governor Gavin Newsom has commuted the death sentences of all 548 inmates currently on the state's death row, changing their punishments to life in prison without the possibility of parole. The action affects the largest death row population in the Western Hemisphere, which has not seen an execution since 2006.

In a letter explaining his decision, Newsom stated that state-sanctioned killing is incompatible with California's values and that the administration of the death penalty is a systemic failure prone to convicting innocent people. While acknowledging that the move would anger and hurt some Californians, the term-limited Democratic governor stated that he acted according to his conscience and constitutional clemency authority.

Although the decision is largely symbolic due to a moratorium Newsom placed on executions in 2019, experts note it could save millions of dollars annually in court costs. The announcement comes during Newsom's final months in office, as he has also been discussed as a potential 2028 presidential contender.

Bank of England Raises Interest Rates to 4% Amid Inflation Pressures

The Bank of England has increased its base interest rate from 3.75% to 4%, marking the first rate hike in over three years. Eight of the nine members on the Monetary Policy Committee voted in favour of the increase, with one member advocating for a higher rise to 4.25%. The decision was prompted by inflationary pressures largely driven by the impact of the US-Iran war on global energy and oil prices. Bank Governor Andrew Bailey stated that further increases could be likely if the conflict in the Middle East is not resolved, though committee members expressed varied expectations regarding the future trajectory of rates.

The rate hike has immediate financial implications for consumers and businesses across the UK. Borrowers, including millions of homeowners facing remortgaging costs and those on variable-rate mortgages or loans, will see increased expenses. Conversely, the rise is expected to provide better returns for savers who actively shop around for competitive interest rates. Meanwhile, businesses face higher borrowing costs and potential impacts on employment and wages as companies navigate tighter financial conditions.

The decision comes during a challenging economic climate, coinciding with a cost-of-living squeeze and expected increases in household energy bills. While some analysts and committee members view the move as a necessary preventive measure against persistent inflation, others question the timing during a period when broader price contagion has remained limited and UK economic growth has shown relative resilience.

CVS Health Announces Plan to Separate Retail Pharmacy and Insurance Businesses

CVS Health announced plans to split into two separate, publicly traded companies by the first half of 2028, dismantling its vertically integrated healthcare conglomerate model established following its 2018 acquisition of Aetna. The proposed breakup will separate the company's roughly 9,000 retail pharmacies from its insurance business, pharmacy benefit manager Caremark, and healthcare services units including Oak Street Health and Signify Health.

Under the separation plan, the insurance and pharmacy benefit management enterprise will retain the CVS Health name and continue to be led by CEO David Joyner. Meanwhile, the standalone retail drugstore business will be named CVS Pharmacy and will be led by Prem Shah, currently the group president of CVS Health. Existing CVS shareholders will receive stock in both resulting companies upon completion of the transaction.

The announcement coincided with CVS reporting third-quarter earnings and revenue that exceeded Wall Street expectations, with total sales climbing to $108.40 billion from $102.87 billion in the prior-year period. Despite the earnings beat, CVS shares fell following the news of the planned corporate reorganization, reflecting investor uncertainty regarding the dismantling of the combined retail and insurance model.

Company leadership defended the decision as a strategic maneuver executed from a position of financial strength, following an aggressive turnaround effort that included cost cuts and leadership changes. Supporters note that the separation could allow each distinct entity to focus more effectively on its core operations and market opportunities, whereas market skeptics expressed caution over reversing years of integration and strategy.

Meta, Google, and TikTok Sue California Over Teen Social Media Law

Meta, Google, and TikTok have filed lawsuits against California Attorney General Rob Bonta to block a newly signed state law known as AB 1709. The legislation, signed by Governor Gavin Newsom in September and slated to take effect at the start of the new year, restricts certain features for users under 16, specifically prohibiting autoplay videos and personalized feeds unless platforms disable them and potentially delete non-compliant users' accounts. Violations could result in fines of up to $50,000 per affected user.

The tech companies argue that the law is unconstitutional and infringes upon their First Amendment rights by interfering with editorial decisions regarding content curation and distribution. TikTok compared the practice of curating content based on preferences to methods used by bookstores, television networks, and streaming services. Additionally, the companies assert that the statute is preempted by Section 230.

Digital safety and civil liberties advocates have also raised concerns about the law's broader implications, despite its stated goal of improving teen mental health. Critics, including the Electronic Frontier Foundation, point out that compliance could necessitate extensive age verification processes, which might compromise user privacy and anonymity by requiring the collection of additional data from all users. Furthermore, opponents argue that restricting youth access to these digital spaces could limit their participation in online activism and cut them off from valuable information and community experiences.

Delhi Announces Winter Action Plan as Toxic Air Returns

As winter and the festive season approach, Delhi and its satellite cities are experiencing a familiar return of hazardous air quality. Falling temperatures and slower winds combine with smoke from neighboring crop residue burning, factory emissions, vehicle exhaust, construction dust, and festival firecrackers to trap noxious smog close to the ground. Residents report a grey haze, a smoky smell, eye irritation, and breathing difficulties, with many turning to masks and air purifiers.

In response to the annual crisis, the Delhi government implemented its Winter Action Plan ahead of schedule, running from November 1 to February 28. The measures include restricting older, more polluting out-of-state vehicles, denying fuel to vehicles lacking emission compliance certificates, temporarily banning dust-generating demolitions and outdoor construction, doubling parking fees, and promoting public transit and electric vehicles. Additionally, the government has encouraged offices to allow half of their staff to work from home and introduced staggered office hours.

Experts have commended the early rollout of the Winter Action Plan as a positive step compared to previous years when measures were delayed until air quality reached critical levels. However, critics and specialists argue that seasonal emergency rules alone are insufficient, emphasizing the need for more holistic and long-lasting solutions to address the root causes of the capital's persistent pollution. The effectiveness of the plan also serves as a crucial test for the governing Bharatiya Janata Party, which pledged to tackle pollution during its election campaign last year.
