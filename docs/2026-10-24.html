Israel Locks Down West Bank and Launches Raids Following Fatal Shooting of Two Soldiers

Two Israeli soldiers were killed and three others wounded when two Palestinian gunmen opened fire near a military checkpoint and the settlement of Telem outside Hebron. Israeli troops returned fire, killing both assailants, whom the military reported were carrying a large quantity of ammunition. The shooting prompted Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to declare a full lockdown of the occupied West Bank and review preparations for an extensive military operation.

Following the attack, Israeli forces imposed cordons around Hebron and the nearby city of Halhul, shutting down nearly 1,000 checkpoints and access gates across the territory. Operations quickly widened, with soldiers raiding at least 15 towns across the northern West Bank—including Jenin, Nablus, and Qalqilya—and detaining dozens of Palestinians. In Hebron, military bulldozers destroyed a three-storey residential building, and hundreds of Palestinian families in the Ein Deir Bahha area were ordered to evacuate as troops surrounded the neighbourhood of one of the suspects.

The escalation occurs just days ahead of an Israeli general election and amidst heightened tensions surrounding the annual olive harvest. Israeli ministers, including far-right members of the coalition, have called for intense military action comparable to operations in Gaza and the dismantling of the Palestinian Authority. In contrast, Palestinian representatives and rights groups have sharply criticized the closures, demolitions, and forced evacuations, describing them as unlawful collective punishment exacerbated by political incitement.

Key facts

  • Two Israeli soldiers and two Palestinian gunmen were killed, and three soldiers wounded, in a shooting near Hebron.
  • Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered a lockdown across the West Bank, closing nearly 1,000 checkpoints and gates.
  • Israeli troops demolished a three-storey building in Hebron, ordered evacuations, and detained dozens of Palestinians across at least 15 towns.
  • Netanyahu announced plans for an extensive military campaign, potentially the largest in the West Bank in decades.
  • The events occurred four days before Israel's general election amid broader seasonal tensions over the olive harvest.

France Deploys 70,000 Police Officers Ahead of Nationwide Demonstrations

France is deploying approximately 70,000 police officers nationwide ahead of planned protests on Saturday, following widespread unrest and looting that erupted during demonstrations the previous weekend. The government's security response includes regular officers reinforced by elite tactical units, mounted police, water cannon, helicopters, and drones. Authorities stated that the heightened posture is intended to deter radical groups, looters, and vandals following the violence of October 17, which saw thousands of stores damaged and hundreds of individuals subsequently jailed through fast-track proceedings.

The demonstrations began as student protests seeking better facilities, more teachers, and overall improvements to the national education system. The movement has since widened into a broader anti-government mobilization driven by public frustration over the rising cost of living and purchasing power. It has drawn participation from trade unions such as the CGT, student organizations including UNEF, and members of the revived Yellow Vest movement. Although President Emmanuel Macron met with high school student representatives on Friday, organizers reported that they were not satisfied and urged supporters to maintain their mobilization.

Ahead of Saturday's events, French authorities implemented strict and varied limits on public gatherings. Demonstrations were restricted to those organized by recognized labor unions, while several cities, such as Lyon, Nice, and Montpellier, issued broad bans on protests. In Paris, police approved only a single union march along a route circumventing the city center, while curfews were maintained in certain central squares. Anticipating possible disturbances, major Paris landmarks including the Louvre, Orsay, and Picasso museums closed for the day, a Paris Saint-Germain football match was rescheduled, and local business owners boarded up storefronts.

Gunmen Kill Seven and Injure Dozens in Shooting at Church Funeral in Jordan

On Friday, gunmen opened fire during a funeral service at an Orthodox church in Hmoud, located in Jordan's Karak governorate, killing seven people and injuring at least 34 others. According to witnesses and officials, between 150 and 200 people were gathered inside when the armed attackers stormed the grounds and discharged weapons before fleeing the scene in a vehicle. Twenty-seven of the injured were transported to local hospitals for medical treatment.

Jordanian authorities swiftly denounced the assault as an act of terrorism. Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya labeled the perpetrators a "group of extremists" and described the incident as an unprecedented tragedy in the kingdom's history. King Abdullah II visited wounded victims at the hospital, pledging that the state would retaliate with the "harshest penalties" and "strike with an iron fist," while asserting that attempts to divide Jordanians through violence would fail against national unity.

Violent gun attacks are rare in Jordan, a Muslim-majority country with a Christian minority of several hundred thousand, including a notable presence in the Karak region. The shooting drew immediate international condemnation from foreign leaders and diplomats, including the Lebanese presidency, the United Nations, and the United States, who denounced the assault on a place of worship.

Kenya Reports Second Ebola Death as Regional Outbreak Expands Across Borders

Kenya has recorded its second death from Ebola after a 65-year-old woman died in quarantine in Nairobi, marking the country's first-ever outbreak of the virus. The victim was a close relative and direct contact of Kenya's first fatal case—a man who traveled from the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and died on October 5. According to Kenya's health ministry, seven other confirmed patients, including three family members, three healthcare workers, and a hospital cleaner, remain in isolation facilities across two locations, with all cases confined to a single cluster.

The epidemic, which originated in the DRC in May, is caused by the Bundibugyo species of Ebola. Unlike the more common Zaire strain, there is currently no approved vaccine or specific medical treatment for Bundibugyo, though clinical trials have begun. The outbreak has become the most geographically widespread in history, spanning four nations: the DRC, Uganda, Kenya, and Nigeria. In the DRC, cases have surpassed 9,700 and deaths have exceeded 4,600, leading the World Health Organization (WHO) to designate the situation a public health emergency of international concern.

Kenyan authorities have maintained that there is no evidence of community spread within the country and have instituted strict screening protocols for passengers and aircraft arriving from the DRC and nine other high-risk regional nations. Meanwhile, international public health bodies are working to track cross-border chains of transmission, investigating potential exposure points such as international airports while accelerating human vaccine trials in the DRC.

Key facts

  • A 65-year-old woman in quarantine became Kenya's second Ebola fatality, both linked to an initial imported case from the DRC.
  • Seven additional patients—three family members, three healthcare workers, and one cleaner—are under treatment in isolation.
  • The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo species of Ebola, for which no approved vaccine or cure exists.
  • The epidemic has affected four African nations (DRC, Uganda, Kenya, and Nigeria), with DRC fatalities exceeding 4,600.
  • Kenya has mandated digital health screening and QR codes for incoming travellers from ten designated high-risk countries.
