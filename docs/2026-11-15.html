SpaceX successfully catches Starship rocket upper stage with mechanical arms

SpaceX has successfully caught the upper stage of its Starship rocket using mechanical arms at its launch site in Texas. The milestone occurred on Saturday following the rocket's 15th test flight, during which the spacecraft orbited Earth 14 times over 22 hours and deployed 26 Starlink satellites. The successful recovery of the spacecraft, nicknamed the Ship, represents a significant step forward in the company's efforts to develop a fully reusable rocket system designed to reduce mission costs and support future missions to the Moon and Mars.

Despite the success with the upper stage, the test flight also encountered a setback regarding the lower stage. SpaceX had intended to catch the Super Heavy booster as well, but that recovery attempt was aborted shortly after launch on Friday. Engineers reported that the booster's engines stopped firing too soon and its guidance system initiated a self-destruction protocol, causing the stage to explode over the Gulf of Mexico. SpaceX has previously caught booster stages on three earlier test flights and is currently investigating the cause of the booster malfunction.

The achievement drew praise from industry experts and space agencies, including NASA, which is paying billions of dollars for Starship to land Artemis astronauts on the Moon. Analysts noted that while the mid-air catch is a major technical breakthrough, achieving repeated successes will be necessary to prove the system's readiness for sensitive payloads and regular operations.

US Flight Suspensions Linked to Copper Wire Upgrade and Routing Issue

A nationwide US ground stop that caused significant travel disruptions and thousands of flight cancellations was linked to a technical issue following a copper wire upgrade. Transport Secretary Sean Duffy stated during a news conference that the incident occurred as contractor L3Harris was replacing outdated copper wire with fibre-optic cable as part of an infrastructure modernization effort. According to officials, a router being worked on began flooding other air traffic control systems, creating communication issues with aircraft and prompting a four-hour suspension on safety grounds.

The four-hour ground stop, which began on Friday afternoon, resulted in more than 15,000 delayed or cancelled flights on Friday, with thousands more delays and cancellations carrying over into Saturday as airlines worked to clear the backlog. The disruption stranded passengers, affected international travel, and led to the rescheduling of various sporting events. Officials, including Federal Aviation Administration Administrator Bryan Bedford, confirmed that there was zero evidence of a cyber-attack, malicious activity, or artificial intelligence involvement.

Investigations are ongoing to determine whether the root cause was installer or user error by the contractor or faulty equipment, with Duffy noting that the contractor "didn't meet the moment." Officials also faced questions regarding why complex updates were scheduled during busy Friday afternoon hours rather than overnight. In response to the crisis, Transportation Secretary Duffy urged Congress to approve substantial additional funding to accelerate infrastructure upgrades and prevent future occurrences.

Italy Fines Meta €805 Million Over Unauthorized Access to Android Photos

Italy's Data Protection Authority has imposed an €805 million fine on Meta for accessing the photos and videos of Android users without specific consent. According to regulators, the privacy infringement involved a Facebook app feature that suggests pictures from a user's personal gallery for posts, utilizing a single app-wide authorization without properly informing approximately 20 million smartphone users about how their data was being used.

The Italian authority stated that the violation affected a high number of individuals and remains ongoing for users who have not updated the app or who previously granted system permissions. Meta has been given 60 days to disable the feature by default and obtain separate consent from users, alongside facing criticism for a lack of cooperation during the investigation which began in July 2025.

Meta strongly disagreed with the decision, calling the fine disproportionate and maintaining that the feature is a straightforward tool to help users share content, with permission granted at the device level. The company announced plans to appeal the penalty. Additionally, Meta had previously argued that the case should fall under Irish legal jurisdiction given its European headquarters in Ireland, though Italian regulators rejected this claim.

While the judge's order keeps the park open and accessible to the public—including paths, courses, and picnic areas—it notes that the administration is not required to maintain golf operations during the litigation. However, the government cannot use the absence of golf operations as a justification to exclude the public.

This decision marks the latest legal hurdle for President Trump's various capital redesign and construction initiatives, which have frequently faced court challenges. Judge Reyes has presided over combative litigation regarding the park, previously issuing a narrow ruling that blocked the National Park Service from cutting down trees without advance notice. The administration maintains that the existing course is poorly designed and dangerous, asserting that the planned upgrades will greatly enhance the dignity and beauty of Washington, D.C.

SpaceX successfully caught the upper stage of its Starship rocket using mechanical arms at the launch tower for the first time, following a 22-and-a-half-hour test flight. While the upper section—referred to as the Ship—successfully reached space, deployed 26 Starlink satellites, completed 14 orbits, and returned to be secured by the tower's mechanical arms (nicknamed "chopsticks"), the recovery of the lower stage was aborted. Catching both parts of the rocket reliably is central to SpaceX's ambitions to create a fully reusable launch system, building on the success of its earlier Falcon 9 rocket recoveries. Experts described the successful recovery of the upper stage as a substantial step forward despite the setback with the first stage booster, noting that the company has now demonstrated both halves of the recovery challenge separately.
