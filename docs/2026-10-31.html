Binance Targeted in Record $6.3 Billion Cryptocurrency Hack Linked to North Korea

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has been hit by a massive security breach resulting in approximately $6.3 billion being accessed in what is believed to be a record digital asset heist. The exchange initially suspected that a DevOps engineer's compromised laptop allowed unauthorized withdrawals, but co-CEO Richard Teng later stated that subsequent investigations uncovered broader breach points and that attackers utilized a self-destruct mechanism to erase tracks and tooling.

Blockchain analytics firms, including TRM Labs and Chainalysis, have identified the Lazarus Group—also known as TraderTraitor, an elite state-backed North Korean hacking unit—as the prime suspect with high confidence. Experts note that North Korea has systematically built its cyber operations to generate foreign currency, bypass international isolation, and fund military and weapons of mass destruction programs.

In response to the incident, Binance stated that it has frozen around half of the stolen funds, secured about $3 billion, and set aside over $6.34 billion in company funds to cover all user losses, assuring users that their money remains safe. Law enforcement agencies, including the FBI and the US Secret Service, are actively working to trace and recover the remaining assets and bring the perpetrators to justice. The security breach also triggered a broader market reaction, causing declines in cryptocurrency values and related financial service stocks.

  • Binance suffered a security breach involving approximately $6.3 billion in digital assets.
  • Blockchain analytics firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis linked the hack to the North Korean state-backed Lazarus Group.
  • Binance stated it has secured around $3 billion of the stolen funds and set aside over $6.34 billion to cover losses.
  • The US Secret Service and the FBI are assisting with investigations and recovery efforts.
  • The incident caused a downturn in cryptocurrency markets, with Bitcoin and shares of related platforms experiencing price drops.

US approves $2.1bn sale of Patriot missiles to Ukraine funded by European allies and Canada

The United States has approved the potential sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 interceptor missiles to Ukraine in a package valued at $2.1 billion. Announced by the US State Department, the deal stands as one of the most significant defensive weapons transactions since the start of President Donald Trump's second administration in January 2025. The purchase includes missiles, parts, and support, with the American firm Lockheed Martin serving as the principal contractor.

While Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy welcomed the announcement, he cautioned that it could take "several years" for the deliveries to arrive, noting that weapons sales of this scale typically take a long time to fulfill and might not reach Ukraine until the early 2030s. Zelenskyy stressed that Kyiv urgently needs the interceptors immediately to counter escalating Russian aerial attacks, pointing to recent intensified strikes on critical energy infrastructure ahead of winter and attacks on civilian areas.

The purchase is funded entirely by Kyiv's allies rather than US funds, utilizing contributions from Canada, Germany, and Norway via the JUMPSTART program, alongside national funds financed by the European Union's Ukraine Support Loan. The State Department stated that the move supports US foreign policy and national security objectives by enhancing the stability of a European partner. However, the approval follows recent tensions between Washington and Kyiv regarding delayed shipments, US sanctions easing tied to a Russian diesel deal, and concerns regarding domestic weapons inventories resulting from heavy munitions use and support for other operations.

Key facts

  • The US approved the sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 interceptor missiles to Ukraine for an estimated $2.1 billion.

  • Lockheed Martin is the principal contractor for the manufacturing of the missiles.
  • Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that deliveries could take several years and may not arrive until the early 2030s.
  • The Patriot system is highlighted as an effective weapon against fast-moving ballistic missiles, which Ukraine says it urgently needs to defend against intensified Russian strikes on energy infrastructure.

Two Foreign Nationals Die of Suspected Electrocution in Bengaluru Hotel Pool

Two foreign nationals died on Friday evening from suspected electrocution in the swimming pool of the Conrad Hotel, located on the third floor in the Ulsoor or Halasuru area of Bengaluru. The deceased were identified as Alexander Maximilian Theiss, a 57-year-old German national and Lufthansa crew member, and Juraj Horvat, a 45-year-old Slovak national and IBM project manager.

Two other individuals were injured while attempting to rescue the men. Mathew Desantis, a 41-year-old American tourist staying at the hotel, reportedly detected an electric current while swimming and managed to reach safety, though he experienced a headache afterward. Rahul Tiwari, a 28-year-old hotel pool attendant and lifeguard, jumped into the water to assist and sustained injuries. Both survivors were transported to Manipal Hospital for treatment.

Medical staff at Manipal Hospital reported that Theiss was brought in dead, while doctors attempted unsuccessfully to resuscitate Horvat. According to Dr. Mabel Vasnaik, head of emergency medicine, the cause of death was electrocution. The two surviving individuals were reported to be in stable condition and out of danger, with plans for discharge following medical evaluations.

Local law enforcement authorities have registered a case regarding the incident and launched an ongoing investigation to determine the exact circumstances and cause of the electrical hazard. The event highlights concerns over safety standards and electrical maintenance within commercial hospitality venues.

Federal Judge Bars Justice Department From Threatening Election Workers Over Ballot Processing

A federal judge has issued an order barring the US Department of Justice from threatening or coercing election workers to stop processing ballots. The late-Friday ruling also prohibits the Justice Department from demanding that local election officials segregate or refrain from counting ballots that the federal government considers potentially cast by ineligible voters. This decision represents a further setback for the Trump administration's attempts to exert control over state-run and local elections.

The legal conflict stems in part from administration allegations that California sent ballots to approximately 20,000 individuals who may not be US citizens, demanding that those ballots be set aside pending citizenship verification. Conversely, California officials maintain that the target list is unreliable and likely includes many eligible citizens. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals recently rejected a Justice Department request to halt California's ballot counting, with a panel ruling that attempting to segregate specific ballots would carry potentially catastrophic effects on the election system.

While representatives for the Justice Department did not immediately respond to requests for comment regarding the specific ruling, the agency previously stated that enforcing federal prohibitions against noncitizen voting is its designated responsibility. Meanwhile, local federal prosecutors have warned that election officials who knowingly process noncitizen ballots could face legal jeopardy. The unfolding legal disputes continue to affect election administration as millions of citizens cast votes in midterm elections.
