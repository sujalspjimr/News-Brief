Mexican drug lord Fausto Isidro Meza Flores arrested in shoot-out

Mexican special forces arrested Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, also known as "El Chapo Isidro," during a raid in Guasave, Sinaloa, following a violent shoot-out. Meza Flores, who was on the FBI's 10 most wanted list and faced a reward of up to $5m, is the alleged leader of the Meza Flores Organisation and associated with the Beltrán Leyva criminal organisation.

The operation relied on US intelligence and involved elite military forces. During the raid, security personnel faced gunfire and explosives. The resulting clashes and subsequent retaliation led to the deaths of seven suspects and four Mexican army members, alongside widespread panic, armed clashes, vehicle fires, and roadblocks across Sinaloa.

Eight individuals in total were detained, including Meza Flores's second-in-command and his brother-in-law, who served as a financial operator. Meza Flores and two other top suspects were flown to Mexico City to face prosecution, while the United States is seeking his extradition on drug trafficking and organized crime charges.

Deadly Fire at Indonesian Confectionery Factory Leaves at Least Three Dead and Dozens Missing

A severe fire broke out at a confectionery factory in Indonesia's Banten province, located about 50 kilometers west of Jakarta, leaving at least three people dead and 36 others missing. The blaze began on Saturday afternoon at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia facility in the Serang district, which produces milk-based snacks and sweets such as Milkita.

According to the National Search and Rescue Agency and regional officials, approximately 60 people were believed to be inside the three-storey factory when the fire started. Firefighters and joint search-and-rescue teams, including the Indonesian Red Cross, continued battling remaining flames and cooling operations into Sunday, while focusing efforts on searching parts of the heavily damaged building where workers were thought to be trapped. Officials reported that 15 survivors had been located, with four individuals taken to a nearby hospital and a local clinic for injuries.

Relatives of the missing workers gathered outside the factory entrance to await updates from authorities. Fire officials noted that the building was already heavily engulfed in flames when crews arrived around 4:30 local time on Saturday, making it difficult to immediately determine whether the facility's internal protection systems functioned properly. The cause of the fire remains under investigation by local disaster agencies and authorities.

UN COP31 Climate Summit Enters Overtime Amid Divisions Over Draft Agreement

The United Nations COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, has entered its second day of overtime as nearly 200 delegations struggle to reach a consensus on a final global climate deal. Co-hosted by Turkey and Australia, the conference missed its original Friday deadline following intense friction over the exclusion and inclusion of specific terms in successive draft agreements.

A new draft text, published early Sunday, omits direct references to "transitioning away from fossil fuels," a point of contention that previously sparked threats of walkouts from the European Union and Pacific states. Instead, the document references past commitments made three years ago to move away from oil, gas, and coal. For the first time, the draft acknowledges that the world is on track to exceed the critical 1.5 degrees Celsius warming limit, while calling for the accelerated development of carbon-removal technologies.

Reactions to the new text are sharply divided. While summit president and Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum expressed hope for a swift breakthrough and claimed the text addresses key concerns, the European Union views the latest iteration as a step backward. Environmental groups have also criticized the omission of explicit fossil fuel phase-out language and the reliance on carbon capture. Meanwhile, negotiations continue as delegations weigh objections and review the final package, with the risk that a failure to reach consensus could suspend the talks until next year.

South Korea's Education Minister Resigns Following AI Cheating Scandal and Exam Backlash

South Korea's education minister, Choi Kyo-jin, resigned on Sunday following an artificial intelligence-assisted cheating scandal that emerged just days before the country's crucial college entrance exam, known as the suneung. Public anxiety heightened after the top scorer in a national tax accountant exam confessed to using a miniature camera and a smartphone to achieve a near-perfect score. The revelation prompted authorities to scramble preventive measures ahead of the high-stakes suneung, which determines university placements and future career prospects in the competitive country.

Minister Choi faced severe public criticism and pressure following a parliamentary hearing where he expressed a lack of confidence in authorities' ability to catch cheaters due to his age, stating, "Due to my age, I am not confident I could." Teachers' unions also criticized the government's response as belated, and Choi notably did not appear at press briefings leading up to the exam, leaving a vice minister to announce new rules such as blocking public Wi-Fi, inspecting clothing items, and mandating that smart devices be handed in.

Despite the controversy, the suneung concluded without major incidents. Officials reported that 111 cases of misconduct involving electronic devices were identified during the exam, with nearly half caught through loud, unsilencable emergency alerts broadcast to phones nationwide. Following the conclusion of the exams, Choi offered his resignation, stating he took full responsibility for failing to prepare adequately in advance. President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation.

Speeding Truck Collision in Gujarat Kills Seven Members of Army Families

A fatal traffic collision occurred on Saturday night along the Bhuj-Khavda highway in Gujarat's Kutch district, resulting in the deaths of seven individuals from two families belonging to Uttarakhand's Garhwal region. According to police statements, a Hyundai Aura carrying the victims collided head-on with an oncoming trailer truck between 9:30 pm and 10 pm while travelling from Khavda toward Bhuj. The impact was severe enough to split the car in half. Despite being rushed to the hospital via ambulance, all seven occupants succumbed to their injuries.

The victims included two serving Indian Army personnel, their wives, and three young children. The families had traveled to Kutch to visit the Rann and were returning when the tragedy struck. The deceased were identified as Saurabh Singh Negi, his wife Savita, and their three-month-old daughter Sivika; Rajesh Singh Rawat and his seven-year-old daughter Rakshita; and Rinki Nitin Gosai and her four-year-old daughter Vamika. Negi and Rawat were stationed in Bhuj.

In response to the tragedy, Prime Minister Narendra Modi expressed public condolences and announced financial relief. The Prime Minister's Office stated that compensation of ₹2 lakh would be provided from the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund (PMNRF) for the next of kin of each deceased person, along with ₹50,000 for any injured parties. Local authorities, including a Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL) team, examined the crash site as police investigations into the incident continued.

International BepiColombo Spacecraft Arrives at Mercury

The international BepiColombo spacecraft has successfully arrived at Mercury after an eight-year journey, marking only the second time in history a mission has entered orbit around the planet closest to the sun. Announced by the European Space Agency (ESA) on November 21, the arrival initiates a months-long series of manoeuvres for the joint European and Japanese mission.

The mission consists of two scientific probes built by ESA and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA). The probes remain conjoined for now, but will separate after reaching the prescribed orbit for JAXA's MIO probe on December 9 to sample the planet's magnetic field and solar wind interactions. Meanwhile, the ESA-built Mercury Planetary Orbiter will gradually descend until March 2027 to continuously study the planet's surface.

Travelling to Mercury requires significant energy to lower the spacecraft closer to the sun without gaining excess speed and overshooting the planet. En route, the mission tested onboard instruments such as the Mercury Imaging X-ray Spectrometer, representing the first X-ray imaging device sent on an interplanetary mission.

Italian Jeweller and Businessman Paolo Bulgari Dies Aged 89

Paolo Bulgari, the prominent Italian jeweller and businessman who helped transform his family's enterprise into a global luxury brand, has died in Rome at the age of 89. His family announced his passing, remembering him as "the greatest jeweller of our time." Bulgari had served as the chairman of the Bvlgari Group since 1984, guiding the company through international growth and diversifying its offerings into watches, perfumes, leather goods, and hotels.

Born into a legacy established by his grandfather Sotirios Voulgaris in Rome during the late 19th century, Paolo Bulgari joined the family business shortly after finishing school. He developed a reputation as one of the world's leading experts in precious stones. Under his leadership, the brand distinguished itself from traditional Parisian jewellery houses through a bold aesthetic characterised by rich yellow gold, generous proportions, unexpected colour combinations, and striking gemstones. Over the decades, the company's creations adorned numerous notable figures, ranging from royalty such as Diana, Princess of Wales, to Hollywood icons including Elizabeth Taylor, Audrey Hepburn, and Marilyn Monroe, as well as contemporary stars like Zendaya and Rihanna.

In 2011, the Bulgari family sold its controlling stake in the company to the French luxury group LVMH in a transaction valued at approximately 3.7 billion euros. Beyond his commercial work, Paolo Bulgari remained active in philanthropy; in 2019, he and his wife established the Paolo Bulgari Foundation to aid young people with schooling and job training in disadvantaged neighbourhoods on the outskirts of Rome. His funeral is scheduled to take place in Rome on Monday, 23 November, at the Basilica of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme.
