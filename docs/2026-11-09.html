Krakow Mass Shooting Suspect Dies in Hospital Following Police Shootout

The 24-year-old suspect in a mass shooting in Krakow, Poland, has died in the hospital from injuries sustained during a shootout with police. The attacker opened fire on Saturday in the city's busy Main Market Square, killing six people—including four Polish citizens, a British national, and an Austrian woman—and injuring numerous others. Armed officers engaged the gunman and shot him within three minutes of the attack starting, an action officials credited with preventing further loss of life.

Following the attack, authorities searched the suspect's family home and discovered the bodies of his parents and sister, whom police later confirmed were killed prior to the market square shooting. Ministers and early investigations indicated that the 24-year-old Polish man was reportedly inspired by far-right ideologies and ultra-nationalism, prompting prosecutors to launch a terror investigation led by a specialized unit.

In the immediate aftermath, two off-duty surgeons who were having lunch near the square rushed to provide critical medical aid before ambulances arrived, helping both injured civilians and the wounded police officers. Two senior constable officers shot during the apprehension subsequently emerged from comas, while the prime minister hailed law enforcement for preventing further deaths. Dublin Civil Defence volunteers who were on a city break also assisted victims with CPR and hemorrhage control.

Pope Leo Criticizes Argentina's Austerity Measures During Visit to Buenos Aires

During a visit to Buenos Aires on his Latin America tour, Pope Leo XIV delivered a pointed critique of President Javier Milei's strict austerity measures. The pontiff asserted that the economy must serve the people rather than forcing people to serve the economy, highlighting the difficulties faced by the working poor, pensioners, and individuals dealing with illness, dependency, and disability. The Pope's remarks were widely interpreted as a reference to the administration's controversial cuts to disability assistance and social spending.

In response to the papal address, President Milei defended his libertarian economic agenda, which relies on cutting public spending to curb inflation. Milei stated that his administration is working to avoid punishing future generations of Argentines to benefit current citizens, emphasizing that the constitutional rights to life, liberty, and property form the foundation of Western democracies. While Milei previously hurled harsh insults at Pope Leo's predecessor, Pope Francis, he struck a conciliatory tone during the current visit, praising Francis as an exceptional historical figure and welcoming the reigning pontiff with enthusiasm.

The papal visit serves as the first papal trip to Argentina in nearly forty years and unfolds amid deep political and social divisions within the country. Supporters of the church and opponents of the government hope the visit will help heal societal fractures and bring relief to vulnerable populations, while the administration maintains that its fiscal reforms are necessary to secure long-term economic stability.

  • Pope Leo XIV arrived in Buenos Aires as part of a 12-day tour of Latin America, following recent lung cancer surgery.

Train Collision in Egypt Leaves Several Dead and Injured

At least seven people have been killed and more than 100 injured following a collision between two passenger trains south of Cairo, Egypt. The incident occurred on Sunday in the al-Ayyat district of Giza province when a train traveling from Giza to Fayoum crashed into the rear of a stationary service heading from Cairo to Aswan. The impact caused several carriages to derail, flip, and fall down an embankment, while local residents and emergency services rushed to the scene to pull survivors from the wreckage.

Egyptian President Abdul Fattah al-Sisi stated that he ordered state authorities to investigate the circumstances of the accident and hold negligent parties accountable. Area hospitals were placed on heightened alert to deal with the massive influx of patients suffering from injuries ranging from broken bones to head trauma, with the public prosecutor warning that the death toll could potentially rise.

The accident has reignited scrutiny over Egypt's transportation safety and infrastructure. While the government has invested billions of dollars in recent years to modernize its railways and transportation networks, the country's rail system continues to face safety issues and a history of poor maintenance. Calls for accountability and the resignation of the transport minister emerged following the disaster, which took place in the same region as Egypt's deadliest historical train tragedies.

Delhi Air Quality Data Controversy and Political Row Follows Diwali

A political controversy has erupted in Delhi following Diwali celebrations as thick smog enveloped the city and air quality deteriorated near the 'severe' category, with AQI levels surpassing 400 at multiple locations. Official data showed that PM2.5 levels in parts of the capital soared to 66 times the World Health Organization's recommended 24-hour guidelines on Diwali night before declining early Monday morning.

The Aam Aadmi Party and the Congress party have raised serious questions over air quality data management, pointing out that readings from several monitoring stations went blank or became unavailable for hours during the peak pollution period on Diwali night. AAP leaders accused the government of data fraud and manipulation, claiming that monitoring stations were intentionally shut down or that authorities attempted to hide the true extent of the pollution spike. Meanwhile, Congress criticized the administration over the missing data from numerous monitoring stations.

In response, the Bharatiya Janata Party mounted counter-allegations, pointing to a sharp spike in stubble-burning incidents in neighboring Punjab on Sunday evening. BJP leaders alleged that farm fires in Punjab jumped tenfold to roughly 1,200 incidents, characterizing the sudden surge as a deliberate conspiracy orchestrated to worsen Delhi's air quality and target the festival.

Additionally, analyses of Central Pollution Control Board records noted that fine-dust readings at several monitoring stations stopped just short of 1,000 µg/m³ while their respective gas monitors continued functioning. The dispute underscores ongoing tensions between political factions over accountability for the capital's persistent post-festival pollution crisis.

Collected samples are being sent to accredited laboratories for analysis, with results anticipated within ten days.

Alongside these regional developments, Kerala authorities acted in response to consumer complaints and public apprehensions regarding potential vehicle damage caused by contaminated fuel. Under a 2014 state notification, designated officials possess the authority to conduct inspections and pursue legal action against violations.

In response to the allegations, public sector oil marketing companies have rejected claims that their retail outlets sold petrol containing up to 31% ethanol. The companies stated that their supplied E20 petrol adheres to the Bureau of Indian Standards specification and raised concerns regarding the sampling procedures and methodology used during the testing exercises.
