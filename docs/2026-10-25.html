Hundreds of Thousands Protest Across France as Clashes Prompt Nearly 1,000 Arrests

Hundreds of thousands of demonstrators took to the streets across France on Saturday to protest rising living costs, growing inequality, and deteriorating school conditions. While daytime marches remained largely peaceful, clashes erupted late in the day between police and groups of radical activists in several major cities, including Paris, Marseille, Lyon, and Bordeaux. Demonstrators erected burning barricades, threw projectiles, and damaged storefronts, prompting riot police to deploy tear gas, baton charges, and water cannons.

French authorities enacted a massive security operation to prevent a recurrence of the intense rioting and vandalism witnessed the previous weekend. More than 70,000 police officers and specialized elite units were deployed across the country, and President Emmanuel Macron had restricted authorized demonstrations solely to those organized by recognized trade unions. Security officials reported that nearly 1,000 arrests were made nationwide on Saturday, and Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez stated that at least two people sustained serious injuries.

Despite the scattered clashes, French officials emphasized that the level of disorder remained far below the scale of the preceding week, during which roughly 1,500 shops were looted, buildings were torched, and 2,700 people were detained. Authorities noted that proactive policing averted multiple looting incidents, even as underlying public frustration over economic pressures continues to mount against the government.

Key facts

  • Between 410,000 (interior ministry estimate) and 800,000 (trade union estimate) people protested across France over living costs and school conditions.
  • Nearly 1,000 people were arrested nationwide, with interior minister Laurent Nuñez reporting that at least two people were seriously injured.
  • A massive security force of over 70,000 police officers and elite units was deployed nationwide to prevent widespread looting and arson.
  • Police used tear gas and water cannons against masked demonstrators who threw projectiles, torched barricades, and damaged property in Paris, Marseille, Lyon, and Bordeaux.
  • Property damage was substantially lower than the previous weekend, when an estimated 1,500 shops were looted and 2,700 people were arrested.

Ukraine Reports Restoration of Starlink Speeds as Musk Alleges Terms of Service Violations

Ukrainian officials announced that Starlink satellite connectivity has stabilized following roughly ten days of severe service limits. Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara stated that connection speeds over 400 km/h are now achievable, indicating that operational capacity has been restored for frontline troops, communication networks, logistics, and drone operations. Previous speed caps of around 160 km/h (99 to 100 mph) had disrupted Ukrainian combat missions, impaired efforts to intercept Russian attack drones, and prevented strikes behind enemy lines.

Following Ukraine's announcement, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk stated publicly that the restrictions were put in place because the Ukrainian military had breached Starlink's terms of service by employing the network for offensive drone strikes. In response, Ukrainian presidential adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov stated on Telegram that Ukraine is physically unable to use the Starlink network for medium- and long-range attack drone operations.

The service disruptions emerged amid heightened diplomatic tensions between Washington and Kyiv over Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian oil refineries, which coincided with a US agreement to import Russian diesel. Controversy also persisted regarding who initiated the restrictions; Musk stated that the military Starshield system is controlled by the US government and military, whereas US Secretary of State Marco Rubio stressed that SpaceX is an independent private enterprise. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy argued that the limits were directed by the US government and technically executed by SpaceX.

Key facts

  • Ukrainian Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara confirmed Starlink connectivity is stable, with speeds exceeding 400 km/h restored.
  • Previous restrictions capping terminal speeds at 160 km/h (99-100 mph) led to several failed Ukrainian combat missions and disrupted drone operations.
  • SpaceX CEO Elon Musk alleged Ukraine breached terms of service by using Starlink for offensive drone strikes, claiming a loophole was closed.
  • Ukrainian presidential adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov stated Ukraine is physically unable to utilize Starlink for medium- and long-range attack drones.
  • SpaceX and US officials exchanged conflicting statements over whether the US government or SpaceX was responsible for enforcing the Starshield limits.

In favour / positives

  • Musk defended the network limitations as necessary enforcement of Starlink's terms of service against using civilian satellite technology for offensive lethal strikes. The Guardian

Brazil Holds Tense Presidential Runoff Between Incumbent Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro

Brazilians are voting in a closely fought presidential runoff election between incumbent leftist President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and right-wing Senator Flávio Bolsonaro. Flávio Bolsonaro, the 45-year-old son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, finished first in the October 4 opening round with 47% to Lula's 45%, leading by more than 2.2 million votes. Since Brazil's return to democracy in the 1980s, no candidate who trailed after the first round has overturned the deficit to win the runoff, though recent opinion polls showed the two candidates running neck-and-neck.

Flávio Bolsonaro has campaigned on promises to implement an iron-fist crackdown on organized crime and violence, cut taxes, reduce public spending, and lower interest rates. He has also pledged that his first act if elected would be to free his father, Jair Bolsonaro, who is currently serving a 27-year sentence under house arrest for plotting a coup following his 2022 defeat. Bolsonaro supporters argue that the country needs wholesale change after roughly 16 years of governance under Lula's Workers' Party.

Lula, who is seeking a fourth non-consecutive term as he turns 81, has framed the contest as a battle over democratic norms and national sovereignty, accusing the United States of electoral interference—a claim Washington described as baseless. Lula's campaign mobilized massive rallies, including a gathering of roughly 1.1 million people on Rio de Janeiro's Copacabana beach, to drive turnout among the 33 million citizens who abstained in the first round. His supporters emphasize his record of reducing unemployment, lowering inflation, expanding welfare, and overseeing four-decade lows in Amazon deforestation, while warning against a return to Bolsonarismo.

Key facts

  • Flávio Bolsonaro led the October 4 first round with 47% of the vote against Lula's 45%, a margin of over 2.2 million votes.
  • Nearly 159 million Brazilians are eligible to vote in the Sunday runoff election.
  • Incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, turning 81, is seeking a fourth non-consecutive presidential term.
  • Flávio Bolsonaro has promised spending cuts, a crackdown on crime, and the release of his father, Jair Bolsonaro.
  • Former president Jair Bolsonaro is serving a 27-year sentence under house arrest for an attempted coup following the 2022 election.

Authorities in Andhra Pradesh are preparing for a very severe cyclonic storm, while Odisha has activated its disaster mitigation machinery. Farther inland, states including Jharkhand anticipate rainfall, lightning, and wind gusts reaching up to 50 kmph on October 28 and 29.

In Tamil Nadu, the Greater Chennai Corporation deployed pumping stations and motorized drainage to address waterlogging in multiple wards, while establishing an emergency helpline for tree falls and civic issues.
