Russia-Ukraine Peace Talks Conclude in Abu Dhabi Without Breakthrough, Agree on Prisoner Exchange

Trilateral talks involving Russia, Ukraine, and the United States concluded in Abu Dhabi after two days of negotiations regarding post-war security architecture and an end to the nearly five-year conflict. Although the meetings ended without a major breakthrough, both sides confirmed that a large-scale prisoner of war exchange has been agreed upon and is expected to be carried out soon.

The discussions focused heavily on European security frameworks. Kyiv seeks reassurances of allied support if attacked again despite not being in NATO, while Moscow aims to limit NATO's influence in eastern Europe. Ukrainian negotiators described the talks as extremely difficult due to strict Russian directives, whereas Kremlin representatives placed conditions on future progress depending on the positions of Ukraine, the US, and Europe.

Following the conclusion in Abu Dhabi, US mediators Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff traveled to Moscow to meet with Russian President Vladimir Putin. They subsequently planned to travel to Dublin, Ireland, to speak with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky amidst ongoing conflict and reported strikes.

Spain Sees First General Strike in 14 Years Amid Housing Protests and Unrest

Hundreds of thousands of workers and citizens took to the streets across Spain in the country's first general strike in 14 years, organized by major trade unions to protest soaring housing costs, rising rents, and the high cost of living compared to stagnant wages. The nationwide mobilization caused severe disruptions to public services, transport, and businesses in major cities including Madrid and Barcelona.

While the vast majority of demonstrators participated in peaceful marches, sporadic clashes erupted in the evening in Barcelona and Madrid. Masked and hooded individuals set fire to rubbish bins, launched fireworks at police, and targeted businesses, including estate agencies and retail stores. In response, police deployed riot gear, tear gas, and rubber bullets, resulting in dozens of arrests nationwide amid incidents of road blockades and property damage.

The widespread protests and subsequent strike were catalyzed by public anger over affordability issues, notably highlighted by the recent eviction of an elderly resident in Madrid who later died. The crisis has taken center stage in national politics, prompting an early parliamentary election scheduled for November 29.

Democrat Scott Colom wins historic US Senate seat in Mississippi

Democrat Scott Colom has won the United States Senate race in Mississippi, defeating incumbent Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith after a prolonged ballot-counting process that lasted over a week. Colom's narrow victory by fewer than 5,000 votes makes him the first Black US senator from Mississippi since the Reconstruction era in the 19th century, and the state's first new Democratic senator chosen since 1947.

The upset victory in a deeply conservative state that President Donald Trump won by over 20 points in 2024 contributes to a broader national trend in the midterm elections. Driven in part by voter frustrations with the economy and the Iran war under President Trump, Democrats have secured major legislative gains, capturing control of both the House of Representatives and expanding their Senate majority.

Following the final count, Senator Hyde-Smith released a video conceding the election and wishing Colom well, while Colom celebrated his victory with supporters at Tougaloo College. The outcome highlights the impact of grassroots organizing, particularly within Mississippi's Black community, and marks a significant shift in a state that has elected Republicans to the Senate for decades.
