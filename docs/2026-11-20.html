Trump, Putin, and Xi Hold Historic Trilateral Summit in Shenzhen

US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Chinese President Xi Jinping have met for their first-ever trilateral talks in the south-eastern Chinese city of Shenzhen, held on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit.

During the 90-minute closed-door meeting, the three leaders issued a joint "peace declaration." The statement affirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and touched on areas such as increasing petroleum supply and production, reforming the United Nations, and concerns regarding the International Criminal Court's jurisdiction. However, the declaration omitted explicit mention of ongoing conflicts in Ukraine and Iran.

Reactions to the summit were mixed. Supporters and participants praised the engagement as a historic opportunity for dialogue among the world's largest nuclear powers, with Putin describing it as serious collaborative work. Conversely, critics and analysts questioned the substantive outcomes, viewing the gathering largely as a symbolic photo-op or a diplomatic footnote lacking concrete frameworks for denuclearization or peace.

Separately, the Apec summit drew media attention regarding an incident where critics alleged President Trump appeared to doze off during Xi Jinping's speech—an allegation firmly denied by White House officials, who insisted the president was looking down at papers.

UN Court Rules Myanmar Committed Genocide Against Rohingya

The International Court of Justice (ICJ) has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority during military clearance operations in 2017. This verdict marks the first time an international court has found a state culpable of genocide. According to the UN's top court, Myanmar's military acted with the "intention to destroy" a substantial part of the Rohingya population through indiscriminate killings, widespread sexual violence, extreme violence against children, and the deliberate burning of homes and villages.

The landmark ruling was reached by a majority of nine votes to six. The court noted that around 10,000 Rohingya were violently killed and more than 700,000 were forced to flee to Bangladesh, where large numbers remain in refugee camps alongside those still displaced within Myanmar. The case was brought to the ICJ by The Gambia in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention, utilizing its obligation as a state party to prevent and punish genocide despite having no direct connection to the crimes.

For Rohingya refugees living in camps in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, the verdict brought tears of joy, emotional relief, and renewed hope for a safe return to their homeland. However, the ruling also raises pressing questions about what actions the international community will take next to secure justice and accountability, and the judgment carries broader legal significance given its relevance to other international genocide cases, such as the case brought against Israel regarding Gaza.

  • This is the first time an international court has found a state guilty of genocide.
  • The ICJ found that Myanmar's military killed approximately 10,000 Rohingya and forced over 700,000 to flee during 2017 clearance operations.
  • The case was brought to the court by The Gambia in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention.

Cyclone Akvan Makes Landfall Along Odisha Coast

Cyclone Akvan made landfall along the eastern coast of India, crossing the Odisha coast near the port town of Gopalpur. According to the India Meteorological Department (IMD), the landfall process began around 8 a.m. and was completed by 11:20 a.m. The system brought heavy rainfall, rough seas, and strong winds to coastal districts, leading to transport disruptions including the cancellation and rescheduling of numerous trains and the suspension of bus services in Ganjam district.

Ahead of the storm, authorities evacuated roughly 300,000 people from vulnerable areas to safer shelters, with Ganjam district accounting for nearly half of those moved. Officials reported that over 4,000 pregnant women were shifted, and 1,450 babies were delivered at cyclone shelters. The state government deployed hundreds of rescue and relief teams from the Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force, National Disaster Response Force, and Fire Services, operating under a 'Zero Casualty' mission.

Although it was previously forecast to strike as a severe cyclonic storm with higher wind speeds, Akvan weakened considerably before and during landfall due to factors such as vertical wind shear. Odisha revenue and disaster management minister Suresh Pujari stated that no major damage or loss of life had been reported. The IMD noted that the system is expected to continue moving north-northeastwards across Odisha, gradually weakening into a deep depression and subsequently into a depression, while heavy rainfall is forecast to persist in the region over the following days.

The top court had previously reserved its decision on the applications on October 12.

In its legal filings, Tamil Nadu argued that it was seeking its legitimate pro-rata share of water flows during a distress period and requested that an existing backlog of 11 TMC as of October 7 be cleared immediately. The state contended that the burden of hydrological and meteorological distress should not be shifted entirely onto the lower riparian state while Karnataka continues to utilize available storage for irrigation. Conversely, Karnataka maintained that it faced severe agricultural distress and drought conditions due to a rain-deficient year, asserting that its priority must be conserving water for drinking supplies.

After an eight-year journey spanning approximately six billion miles, the joint European-Japanese spacecraft BepiColombo has reached Mercury and is preparing to enter orbit. The mission represents a major milestone in planetary exploration, as Mercury has been little explored previously, with only two prior NASA missions visiting the planet: Mariner-10 in the 1970s and Messenger in 2011. BepiColombo's arrival requires a complex and perilous orbital insertion maneuver to combat the Sun's immense gravitational pull.

The €1.6bn (£1.4bn) mission aims to unlock several major mysteries surrounding the tiny, extreme planet. Researchers hope to understand how Mercury formed so close to the Sun, why it possesses a magnetic field despite its size, what causes the thousands of shallow hollows on its surface, and why ice exists in its permanently shadowed polar craters despite searing daytime temperatures reaching up to 450C or 430C.

To achieve these goals, the spacecraft will separate into two orbiters—the European Mercury Planetary Orbiter and a Japanese partner—which will move into different polar orbits. While the spacecraft arrived at Mercury following an extended journey caused by a propulsion system issue, full science operations are scheduled to commence in April following initial separation and maneuvers in the coming months.
