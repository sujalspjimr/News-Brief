Leftist Lula Wins Historic Fourth Term as Brazil President, Defeating Flávio Bolsonaro

Incumbent leftist President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has secured a historic fourth term in Brazil's presidency following a dramatic runoff election against far-right Senator Flávio Bolsonaro. With 100% of the votes counted, the 80-year-old incumbent received 51.79% of the vote, finishing roughly 4.4 million votes ahead of his 45-year-old opponent. Lula achieved an unprecedented political comeback, marking the first time since the return of democracy in the 1980s that a first-round runner-up has successfully overturned the deficit to win the second vote.

Following the confirmation of the results by Brazil's electoral authority, celebrations erupted among Lula's supporters on the streets of São Paulo, with crowds expressing relief and joy over the victory. Supporters and campaign officials emphasized the win as a crucial defense of democracy, public health, education, and the environment, while turning away from the policies of the extreme right. Lula's campaign focused heavily on driving voter turnout, successfully reducing the abstention rate that had heavily impacted his base during the initial October vote.

Meanwhile, Flávio Bolsonaro refused to concede the election, with his legal team announcing plans to seek an annulment of the results. Echoing actions taken by his father, former president Jair Bolsonaro, after the 2022 election, the senator argued that a supreme court justice's decision to extend voting hours was unacceptable and compromised the election. Critics noted that Bolsonaro's own campaign had also petitioned the electoral court for a time extension.

Serbia's Aleksandar Vucic Concedes Defeat to Student-Led Movement

Serbia's long-standing leader, Aleksandar Vucic, has conceded defeat to a student-led protest movement in snap parliamentary elections following a record-high voter turnout. Speaking at the headquarters of his Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) in Belgrade, Vucic acknowledged that his opponents were projected to win between 135 and 140 seats in the 250-member parliament, compared to roughly 105 seats for the SNS.

The election marked the culmination of nearly two years of widespread demonstrations that began after a tragic train station canopy collapse in the city of Novi Sad in November 2024, which killed 16 people. The disaster galvanized public anger over alleged corruption, negligence, and poor infrastructure standards, ultimately transforming a localized youth protest into a broad anti-corruption political platform known as the Student List.

Following Vucic's concession, joyous celebrations erupted on the streets of Belgrade and Novi Sad, with supporters dancing, honking car horns, and setting off fireworks. The Student List campaigned on promises to depoliticize public institutions, purge rule-breaking officials, free the public broadcaster from political control, and restore citizens' rights, bringing an end to more than a decade of dominance by Vucic and the SNS.
