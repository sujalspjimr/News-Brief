Paraguay's President Santiago Peña Impeached and Arrested Following Bank Collapse

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña was impeached, stripped of his immunity, and placed under house arrest following the sudden collapse of Ueno Bank. The financial crisis froze the accounts of approximately three million depositors, leaving more than half of the country's adult population unable to access their savings and pensions for basic purchases. Protests and clashes with police erupted in the capital of Asunción as public outrage mounted over the freezing of funds and alleged high-level corruption.

Lawmakers unanimously voted to reject Peña's attempted resignation and proceeded with his impeachment to strip him of the legal immunity typically granted to former presidents. Investigators allege that Peña used his political influence to favor Ueno Bank, which expanded rapidly under his administration after he appointed a former bank CEO to head the central bank. The charges against Peña include conflicts of interest, breach of trust, influence peddling, misappropriation of funds, and manipulating regulatory oversight.

Following Peña's removal, Vice-President Pedro Alliana was sworn in as the interim leader until 2028. Alliana promised that the millions of affected citizens will be fully compensated. Meanwhile, prosecutors have opened investigations into Peña and other officials, and legal proceedings are moving forward in the ordinary courts.

  • Paraguay's President Santiago Peña was impeached and placed under house arrest after the collapse of Ueno Bank.
  • The bank collapse froze the accounts of an estimated 3 million depositors, representing about 60% of the country's adult population.

  • Pedro Alliana was sworn in as interim president to serve out the remainder of the term until 2028.

Pope Leo Begins Latin America Tour Three Weeks After Lung Cancer Surgery

Pope Leo XIV has arrived in Uruguay to begin a 12-day, three-nation tour of Latin America, marking his first major international trip since undergoing surgery to remove a lung cancer tumour last month. The 71-year-old pontiff told reporters on his flight that he is in "pretty good health" and felt confident the journey should proceed after medical staff confirmed the cancer had not spread.

The demanding itinerary spans over 20,000 miles and includes stops in Uruguay, Argentina, and Peru. The visit to Peru is highlighted by the Vatican as a "homecoming" due to the Pope's past years spent there as a missionary and bishop. In Uruguay, his first public speech in Plaza Independencia addressed the importance of universal healthcare, drawing on his own recent medical experience to highlight those lacking timely diagnosis and treatment.

The leg of the journey to Argentina carries particular significance and political sensitivity. It marks a return to the homeland of his predecessor, Pope Francis, who never visited the country during his papacy. The visit also brings Pope Leo into contact with Argentine President Javier Milei, whose past criticisms of Pope Francis and current harsh austerity measures have created political tensions, though President Milei has publicly stated that Argentina awaits the visit with "immense joy."

Vatican officials and medical teams have expressed confidence in the Pope's stamina, noting that no extraordinary measures are being taken for his health and describing him as an individual who underwent an operation rather than someone with a permanent medical condition. Observers note the trip is intended to signal a return to a normal schedule following the brief health scare.

Kenyan Doomsday Cult Leader Sentenced to 200 Years in Prison for Mass Murder

A Kenyan court has sentenced doomsday cult leader Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison for the murder of 191 people, consisting of 190 children and one adult. Justice Diana Mochache delivered the sentence, which includes 100 years for homicide and 100 years for offenses related to starvation, following an eight-hour judgment. Mackenzie and 28 accomplices were found guilty of instructing and forcing members of the Good News International church to fast to death so they could meet Jesus before the end of the world.

The case stems from a 2023 investigation when police discovered mass graves in the Shakahola forest near Kenya's coast, eventually recovering more than 400 bodies. Prosecutors had argued for the death penalty due to the extreme scale of the atrocities, but the judge declined, noting that capital punishment had not been carried out in Kenya since 1987 and would eliminate the prospect of rehabilitation. Mackenzie's co-accused received varying prison sentences of up to 160 years.

Throughout the proceedings, Mackenzie and his co-accused pleaded not guilty and asked the court for leniency, maintaining that no one was forced to die. However, evidence presented during the trial showed that guards enforced the fasting orders, and victims who opposed the teachings were beaten to death or buried alive. The tragedy has drawn intense international attention and prompted broader questions regarding state oversight of religious organizations in Kenya.

While no concrete link has officially been established between the threats against airlines and those directed at media institutions, the Irish government described the situation as a likely coordinated campaign of disruption designed to cause fear and distress. Initial Italian airport emails referenced a banned Russian extremist misogynistic group known as "Male State" or "Male Legion," though representatives for the group have denied any involvement. Officials and security analysts continue to investigate whether the widespread incidents stem from decentralized individuals or a centralized, coordinated effort.

Travelers at affected airports expressed various reactions, with many reporting frustration rather than panic as delays mounted across multiple countries. The ongoing security incidents have raised concerns among leaders such as Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin, particularly as Ireland prepares to host high-profile European Union meetings. Authorities emphasize that all threats are being taken seriously despite being treated as malicious hoaxes so far.

Police have recovered perfume bottles from the vehicle and are examining their potential use during body disposal.
