Ukraine Uses Naval Drones to Strike Russian Ships in Libyan Port

Ukraine's security service and armed forces carried out a night-time attack using seven domestically made naval drones against two Russian vessels docked in the port of Benghazi, Libya. The assault sank the Russian missile corvette Merkury, one of Russia's newest vessels, and hit an accompanying dry-cargo ship, the MV Angara, which Ukrainian officials stated was used to transport weapons. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy praised the operation as exemplary, stating that the action demonstrates that the Russian military fleet has no safe routes even far beyond the Black Sea.

The strike, occurring approximately 1,500 to 2,000 miles from the front lines in Ukraine, has raised international concerns regarding the geographic expansion of the conflict. The United Nations mission in Libya expressed deep concern over the attack, and regional officials noted that Libya is not a party to the Russia-Ukraine war. The vessels were berthed in eastern Libya under a cooperation agreement with the forces of Khalifa Haftar, where the ships had stopped to refuel and resupply.

In response, Russia condemned the operation as a dangerous escalation. Russian officials and state media highlighted the strategic value of the Merkury, a stealth-capable corvette singled out previously by President Vladimir Putin as evidence of Russia's naval development. Meanwhile, retaliatory and ongoing long-range exchanges continued elsewhere, including a Russian air attack in Zaporizhzhia that killed two people, heavy overnight drone barrages intercepted by Russian air defenses, and airspace incidents involving reported drone incursions into Poland.

Trump Faces Bipartisan Backlash Over Nvidia CEO Reinstatement Intervention

US President Donald Trump is facing a bipartisan backlash from lawmakers following his intervention that led to the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as the CEO of Nvidia. Huang had previously resigned less than two weeks prior following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy named Alice Huang. Although Nvidia's board had initially rejected calls to reinstate him, the company reversed its decision after receiving input from senior levels of the US government.

President Trump stated in a CNBC interview that he "made the deal" with the tech giant's board and assured them that Huang's return posed no national security threat, remarking that it would be a "threat to lose him." Following the reinstatement, Nvidia's share price jumped 31%, adding nearly $1 trillion to the company's market value and boosting shares across other tech companies and Asian financial markets.

Despite the positive stock market reaction, the presidential intervention has sparked anger among both Republicans and Democrats in Congress. Lawmakers have raised concerns about executive branch involvement in corporate governance, particularly given that Huang is scheduled to appear before Congress next week to face questioning regarding national security concerns surrounding his relationship with the suspected Chinese intelligence operative. China has denied the spying allegations, and the US Commerce Department recently announced plans to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore-based Nvidia customer where Alice Huang served as chief executive.

Ousted Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina Plans December Return to Bangladesh Amid Official Resistance

Former Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina has announced plans to return to the country on December 16, coinciding with Victory Day, which marks the anniversary of the end of the 1971 war of independence. Hasina, who has been living in exile in New Delhi since fleeing a mass uprising in August 2024, has requested that the Bangladeshi government arrange air transport for her return from Delhi to Dhaka. Her supporters describe the choice of date as deeply symbolic and express confidence in her future political leadership.

However, the Bangladeshi government has strongly rejected her proposed return terms, insisting that she is a convicted criminal and a fugitive. State Minister for Foreign Affairs Humaiun Kobir stated that Hasina cannot return as an ordinary commercial passenger and would instead be brought back in handcuffs under an extradition treaty and strict surveillance. Last year, a Bangladeshi court sentenced Hasina in absentia to the death penalty for crimes against humanity stemming from the deadly crackdown on student-led protests that ended her 15-year tenure. Hasina has denied the charges, rejected the verdict, and stated she is prepared to face the legal process upon her return.

The announcement has heightened bilateral tensions between Bangladesh and India, where Dhaka has accused Hasina and New Delhi of supporting recent unrest and terrorist activities carried out by banned Awami League supporters. India's foreign ministry has not immediately responded to the set return date, though it previously noted it was examining Dhaka's extradition request. The ongoing diplomatic and legal standoff underscores deep political divisions as Hasina seeks to reassert her political relevance from abroad.

COP31 Climate Summit Ends Without Consensus Due to Standoff Over Fossil Fuel Phase-Out

The UN COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, concluded without a consensus agreement after running 48 hours into overtime. A major standoff erupted over the conference's overarching cover text, which failed to include explicit language regarding a transition away from fossil fuels.

The European Union, backed by roughly 130 nations including island states and countries like Panama and Ireland, rejected the text, arguing it amounted to a setback and would "greenwash" fossil fuels by failing to align with climate science or the 1.5-degree warming limit. Conversely, a coalition of developing and oil-rich nations—including India, Saudi Arabia, China, and Russia—supported the proposed text or opposed prescriptive phase-out targets. These nations emphasized the continued necessity of fossil fuels for their economic development, while raising concerns about insufficient clarity on adaptation finance and implementation means.

The collapse of the agreement highlights deep global divisions over the future of coal, oil, and gas production. Activists and observers noted that the outcome demonstrates a willingness among some countries to block weak deals, while national leaders face mounting domestic pressures regarding future fossil fuel projects and environmental commitments.

  • The COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended without a consensus agreement after extending 48 hours into overtime.
