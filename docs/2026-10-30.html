Israeli Election Results Show Deadlock as Netanyahu Coalition Falls Short of Majority

Final results from Israel's general election show a political deadlock, with neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc nor the opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot securing a parliamentary majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Netanyahu's coalition finished with 53 seats, while Eisenkot's bloc secured 48 seats. High voter turnout, particularly among Arab voters, propelled the Arab-led Joint List and Ra'am parties to a record 19 seats, positioning them as potential kingmakers in future coalition negotiations.

In the wake of the results, Netanyahu and his Likud party alleged foreign interference, claiming without official backing that Qatar and Turkey provided illegal funding to boost Arab voter turnout and "steal" the election. Qatar's foreign ministry categorically denied the accusations as a dangerous disinformation campaign. Israeli authorities, including the Shin Bet and the Central Elections Committee, stated there was no evidence or basis for allegations of systematic violations of election integrity or the targeting of an entire community.

Opposition leaders and critics have accused Netanyahu of orchestrating a campaign to delegitimize the election results and the Arab-led parties, which could complicate potential efforts by the opposition to form a governing coalition. With neither major bloc holding an outright majority, difficult coalition talks are expected, with potential paths including unity governments, cooperation with Arab parties, or continued political stalemate.

Supreme Court Appoints Justice Ranjana Desai to Mediate ONGC-RIL Gas Dispute

The Supreme Court of India has appointed former apex court judge Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai as the sole mediator in the long-running Krishna-Godavari (KG) basin gas migration dispute. The case involves the Union government, state-run Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC), and a Reliance Industries Limited (RIL)-led consortium which includes BP Exploration (Alpha) Limited and Niko (NECO) Limited. The appointment was made after a Special Bench was informed by Attorney-General R. Venkataramani that direct settlement talks between the parties had failed to yield a viable solution.

The dispute centers on allegations that the RIL-led consortium "migrated" or "siphoned" natural gas from deposits belonging to ONGC into RIL's KG-D6 block off the Andhra Pradesh coast. In July 2018, an international arbitration tribunal rejected the government's claim of USD 1.55 billion against RIL and its partners. Subsequently, the legal battle saw conflicting rulings, including a February 2025 Delhi High Court order that set aside a Single Judge Bench ruling which had upheld the arbitral award in favour of the RIL-led consortium.

During court proceedings, the Bench noted that the dispute is very complex and requires commercial expertise. The Supreme Court requested Justice Desai to handle the mediation with the assistance of two domain experts suggested by the parties. Both the Centre and the RIL-led consortium agreed to the appointment and assured full cooperation to the mediator.

Counsel for RIL has consistently refuted allegations of theft, explaining that the gas movement was a natural physical phenomenon driven by pressure differentials, comparable to osmosis. RIL representatives also emphasized the company's substantial investment of USD 7.4 billion into the ultra-deepwater project, highlighting its significant contribution to domestic gas production.
