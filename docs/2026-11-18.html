Nvidia Reports Soaring Sales and Rejects Calls to Reinstate Former CEO Jensen Huang

Nvidia has reported strong financial results for its latest quarter, with revenue doubling to $115.8bn and profits also doubling to $65bn, even as the company deals with the fallout from the departure of its co-founder and former chief executive, Jensen Huang. During the company's first earnings report since Huang's exit, interim chief executive Colette Kress assured investors that it was "business as usual" and that the firm's rapid growth is expected to continue with another projected 13% rise in revenue next quarter.

Despite the positive financial figures, Nvidia's leadership firmly rejected growing external pressure to bring Huang back. Board chair Harvey Jones stated that the board has no intention of reinstating Huang, who resigned following revelations of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset. The decision comes amidst a "Bring Jensen Back" campaign backed by over 175,000 signatories, Silicon Valley figures like Elon Musk, and US President Donald Trump, who stated he would "certainly not mind" if the former chief executive were given his job back.

The leadership transition also coincides with shifting regulatory and operational landscapes. Kress noted that Nvidia is no longer shipping Hopper data centre semiconductors to China, aligning with new export restrictions issued by the Trump administration on chip exports to overseas data centres. Meanwhile, Huang is scheduled to testify before congressional committees regarding potential national security concerns on December 1, with Nvidia's general counsel set to follow with a closed-door interview.

Waymo Suspends Driverless Taxi Operations on U.S. Freeways Following California Ban

Autonomous vehicle company Waymo has temporarily suspended its driverless taxi operations on freeways across the United States. The suspension follows a ban implemented by the California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which restricted the company's freeway operations in the state effective November 11. State regulators cited an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incidents, including Waymo vehicles entering an active construction zone on U.S. Route 101 and attempting to overtake patrol vehicles on a San Francisco highway.

In response to the California action, Waymo stated that it chose to voluntarily restrict freeway operations across its other operating markets to evaluate its performance. The company emphasized that safety is central to its operations and noted that no injuries or collisions resulted from the recent construction zone encounters. Waymo's driverless taxis remain permitted to operate on regular city streets, where the company serves over half a million trips each week.

Regulators have indicated that the restrictions in California will remain in place until Waymo demonstrates that it has taken effective corrective actions to address the safety deficiencies. Waymo launched freeway rides in late 2025 to improve travel times and connectivity to destinations such as airports, making the suspension a notable setback for its commercial expansion in major metropolitan areas.

United States Loses Measles Elimination Status Amid Major Outbreak

Global health authorities have announced that the United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, marking the return of endemic transmission after 26 years. The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) made the decision after genetic data showed continuous transmission of a single measles genotype for 12 months or more. The announcement coincides with the worst US measles outbreak since 1991, which has resulted in thousands of infections and multiple deaths.

Public health experts attribute the resurgence to persistent gaps in vaccination coverage, driven by pandemic-related disruptions and widespread misinformation. While measles was eliminated in 2000 through intensive vaccination campaigns, achieving community protection requires a 95% vaccination rate. The current outbreak has also affected a larger proportion of adults than is typical, with about 40% of US cases occurring in individuals aged 20 and older, raising concerns because adults face higher risks of serious complications.

The loss of elimination status has intensified political and public health debates. Critics have raised concerns over the handling of public health messaging by federal officials, including Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr and President Donald Trump, citing past confusion sown around vaccine safety. Meanwhile, federal health officials have stated they are working to close vaccination gaps, rebuild public trust, and continue recommending the MMR vaccine to help regain the country's measles elimination status.

  • The ongoing outbreak is the worst since 1991, with sources reporting between 5,400 and 5,700 cases and multiple deaths in 2026.

  • Endemic measles transmission was also re-established in Mexico and Bolivia.
